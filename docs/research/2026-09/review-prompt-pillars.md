# Review request: the seven research pillars of a memory ecosystem for engineering agents

You are reviewing the organising frame of a research and product programme before it is handed to the agents and people who will run it. The frame is seven "pillars". Each pillar has a thesis, a statement of what the team believes today, and a statement of what would change that belief. The pillars will be used as the labelling vocabulary for an intelligence pipeline (every incoming paper, repo or post gets a pillar), as the grouping for a hypothesis register, and as the structure of the team's tracks. Getting them wrong means months of mislabelled evidence and misgrouped experiments.

Be adversarial. Do not summarise the pillars back. Where you agree, say so in one line.

## Context in four sentences

The company is building a memory ecosystem for AI agents that do engineering work: coding, review, incident response. Its MVP is an immutable, positioned event ledger with provenance on every record, a derived graph for lineage and supersession questions, and a retrieval API; that MVP is one component, not the product. The product claim is *authorised, replayable evidence for agents doing engineering work, across the stores a company already has.* Nothing ships on a mechanism unless it beats a baseline on a frozen harness with a predeclared kill criterion.

## The pillars as written

| Id | Pillar | Thesis | What we believe today | What would change our mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Evidence substrate | Raw, positioned, provenance-carrying events are the bank; receipts and replay are the discipline that makes memory auditable | Retaining what was served, with source position and version, is what makes a later reviewer able to reproduce a decision; no surveyed system emits retrieval receipts; point-in-time reads are absent from the memory literature and from every system surveyed | a system that gets reproducible retrieval without retaining served artifacts; evidence that provenance does not reduce downstream errors; a benchmark that measures reproducibility |
| R2 | Update mechanics | Memory writes are proposals; an independent judge accepts, supersedes or rejects; never delete; consolidation and forgetting are unproven | write-time supersede/contradict edges drive knowledge-update accuracy (REALM base 84.7 vs Zep 74.4); read-triggered reconsolidation adds ~2 points; scheduled summarising consolidation has no on/off measurement and its nearest neighbours are negative (Hippo −3.6, Human-Inspired Memory raw 78.4 vs aggressive 48.4); no source shows forgetting helping | a measured on/off result for scheduled consolidation or forgetting at matched budget, in either direction |
| R3 | Read strategy | Task-conditioned read-time curation beats write-time distillation; the graph is an index for lineage, supersession and entity questions, not the default read surface; stop rules over fixed k | JITMem's read-time curation result; MOOSEDev's typed-question result over top-k; Selective Forgetting's null result for graph traversal at matched roots; Jev-Mem's stop rule is unablated | graph lift over hybrid retrieval at matched units and tokens; curation failing on long structured artifacts; a stop rule ablation |
| R4 | Judgment layer | Small typed decision models (yes/no, choice over a closed set, ordinal score) belong at closed-menu decisions, deterministic rules first, one scorer shadowed at a time; never generation, never numeric or temporal reasoning, never alone in an open loop | listwise choice 0.94 vs pointwise 0.87 recall@1 with 30× fewer calls; planner + decider 9/10 vs decider alone 0/10; the larger escalation battery scored below the cheap one on held-out (90.9 vs 95.5); calibration is claimed by the vendor, not shown; one week of a decision model as pass/fail judge: no accuracy change, ~200× cheaper | calibration data; a decision model beating rules on error cost in a build-time setting; escalation helping on the borderline population; the field rules (no "none" option, pruning off) inverting when a wrong retrieval costs a wrong implementation |

Two topics the team previously tracked separately are folded in: typed decision models (a vendor's "System One" model and its open equivalents) into R4, and recursive self-improvement into R5 plus the oversight questions in R6.

## What to review

1. **Are these the right cuts?** Which pillars overlap enough that evidence will be mislabelled between them (R1 vs R7 on snapshots; R2 vs R3 on curation vs consolidation; R5 vs R6 on oversight)? Which cut would you redraw, and how?
2. **What is missing?** Candidates the team considered and left out: security and injection as its own pillar (currently split across R1 and R4); cost and latency economics (currently inside R6); human review and certification workflows (inside R2); ontology and schema (inside R7); multi-agent and shared memory (nowhere). Argue for or against each, and add any you think are absent.
3. **Are the "believe today" statements true as stated?** Challenge the specific numbers and attributions. Name any that overstate the evidence.
4. **Are the "what would change our mind" statements falsifiable and sufficient?** For each pillar, give the single experiment or source that would most efficiently move the belief.
5. **Is R5 a pillar or a product hope?** The team demoted procedural memory from the first twelve weeks. Should it remain a pillar at all, or fold into R2 and R6?
6. **Is R4 too vendor-shaped?** The evidence base is dominated by one vendor's model and its builders. Does the pillar survive if that vendor disappears, and is it stated in a vendor-neutral way?
7. **Ordering.** If the team can only work three pillars deeply this quarter, which three, and why?
8. **Labelling test.** Assign a pillar to each of these incoming items and say where the vocabulary fails: (a) a paper on injection attacks through retrieved memory; (b) a repo that adds a signed versioned catalogue to a vector store; (c) a benchmark for multi-agent shared memory; (d) a vendor post on "dreaming" consolidation triggered by idle time; (e) a paper showing an LLM judge shares failure modes with the system it judges; (f) an MCP memory-server standard proposal.

## Format

Numbered answers matching the questions. Verdict first, then reasoning, bullets over prose. Close with: the pillar table as you would rewrite it, in the same five columns, and one sentence on what the team would most regret about the current version in six months. Under 2,000 words.
