claim: On our own context graph, Jev deciding memory types and typed links at write time (up to 10 candidate neighbours, edges confirmed at score >= 0.60, as in arXiv 2609.23986) matches our current LLM extraction on link accuracy against human labels and on answer quality, at a fraction of the build time and cost, and beats deterministic-only links.
proposed experiment: Arms on one frozen harness, same corpus, same answering model, same retrieval and budget: (A) Jev write-time controller as the paper describes it: deterministic candidate neighbours (vector, lexical, entity, time; at most 10), Jev confirms typed edges (semantic, temporal, causal, entity) plus the PDLC trace links (implements, verifies, refines, satisfies, blocks) at >= 0.60, with timestamps and exact entity ids creating edges directly; thresholds also refit on a held-out labelled slice and reported both ways, raw probabilities logged, model version pinned. (B) our current LLM extraction and linking. (C) deterministic links only (timestamps, exact entity matches). Plus the ablation the paper lacks, run on A: controller on writes only, on reads only (routing and stop rule), and on both. Datasets: our PDLC memory questions (scope note N-SCOPE-20260928-01: recall the decision behind a design, notice a changed requirement, link a pull request to what it implements, warn before a repeated failed fix, spot a stale memory) and LoCoMo for comparability with the paper. Measures: memory build time and cost per 1,000 writes; link precision and recall per link type against human labels (at least 300 sampled link decisions, two labellers, agreement reported; B21 sets the sample needed to bound false links); answer quality on PDLC questions and on LoCoMo, graded by a model from a different family than both Jev and the extractor, with a human-checked subsample; at least 3 runs per arm, spread of repeated B runs as the noise floor. Predeclared margin: A is preferred for linking if its link F1 is within 2 points of B, its build cost is at most one fifth of B, and its PDLC answer quality is not below B by more than the noise floor. Kill: A's trace-link precision below C's, or A's PDLC answer quality below C's. Design warnings kept from the request: the paper reports one benchmark (LoCoMo only, despite "two benchmarks"), one answering model, an unstated grader, no ablations, single runs, different baselines for build speed (Nemori) and latency (MAGMA), and uncalibrated scores behind fixed thresholds (B14). Also test whether neighbouring memories in the state flip Jev's link decisions (overlaps PC-SURVEY-20260928-05, untrusted state text flipping System One decisions: run its perturbation set on arm A) and share the trace-link labels and protocol with PC-SURVEY-20260928-06 (who should decide IMPLEMENTS links); merge where they overlap rather than run twice.
why it matters for us: R9 asks whether a System One model earns its place at a given memory stage on our data; ingestion and linking are the stages with no published evidence beyond this one unablated paper, and the context graph (R10) depends on typed links being right. If Jev matches the extractor on link accuracy at a fifth of the cost we move linking to it with local thresholds; if deterministic links do as well, we keep linking cheap and code-owned and spend the model budget elsewhere.
