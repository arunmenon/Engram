claim: On our coding-agent tasks, serving a short structured record written when a past task closes (bug class, root cause, fix pattern, lesson) raises the pass rate over memory-off, while serving the top-1 raw transcript chunk from the same history does not.
proposed experiment: Frozen coding-agent harness and model, same history of past trajectories, same context budget. Arms: (A) memory off; (B) top-1 raw transcript chunk retrieved from past trajectories by our current retriever; (C) top-1 four-field record (bug class, root cause, fix pattern, reusable lesson) written by a fixed model at trajectory close, without any knowledge of future target tasks (the paper's record was target-aware, which we must not copy); (D) irrelevant record control. Dataset: at least 100 repository tasks from our own repos or SWE-style tasks with executable tests, with at least one related past trajectory each, 4 seeds per task. Metrics: pass rate by executable tests, steps and tokens per task, bootstrap intervals by task. Predeclared margin: C beats A by at least 3 points with the task-bootstrap 95% interval above zero. Kill: C does not beat A (interval includes zero) and does not reduce steps; then procedural memory stays deferred (B16 unchanged). Also record, per failing task, whether the relevant past trajectory was retrieved but its fix pattern was lost (the paper's form-degradation category) to check where B fails.
why it matters for us: This is R6 evidence on our own workload: the paper finds that four memory systems, including Mem0, rarely beat memory-off on coding and that the served record's form, not ranking, is the main failure, which qualifies B16 (raw beats distilled). The result decides whether R5 procedural memory is worth building now and what the write step must produce.
