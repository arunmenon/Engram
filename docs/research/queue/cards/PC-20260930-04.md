claim: On questions that ask what was true at an earlier time, a memory that records "the world changed" as a new dated claim (old claim kept valid for its period) answers at least 10 points more accurately than one that marks the old claim superseded, with no loss on current-value questions.
proposed experiment: arm A single supersedes edge (old claim excluded from recall once replaced) vs arm B typed change (correction removes the old claim for all times; world change keeps the old claim with a computed end; misrecorded invalidates the extraction only), same extractor, same retrieval, same reader · dataset: the knowledge-update fixtures on the frozen harness, extended with paired "what is it now" and "what was it on date D" questions for each changed fact, at least 100 changed facts, with each change labelled correction or world change by hand · metric: accuracy on current-value questions and on as-of-date questions, reported separately, plus the rate at which the change type is assigned correctly · predeclared margin: arm B at least 10 points higher on as-of-date questions and within 2 points on current-value questions · kill: if arm B gains under 5 points on as-of-date questions, or the change type is assigned correctly under 80% of the time, keep the single edge. Design warning: the source is a specification with conformance cases and reports no accuracy; the effect size is unknown.
why it matters for us: R2 holds that updates, supersession and retirement need distinct semantics; this tests the first split that matters for decisions and requirements, which are revised far more often than they are wrong. It also gives B3 a measured contribution for supersede edges in isolation, which the register says is missing.
