claim: On our frozen memory harness, a Jev relevance cutoff (keep score >= 0.8, floor 1) over the same candidate pool finds at least as much gold evidence as fixed top-6 while cutting served tokens, but total cost per correct answer, including rerank calls, is higher at pool size 25 and lower at a smaller pool.
proposed experiment: Arm A fixed top-6 from hybrid retrieval; arm B Jev cutoff 0.8 over the same pool, swept at pool sizes 10, 25; arm C open-weight decision model (Laya fine-tune) with the same cutoff rule. Fetch depth matched across arms. Dataset: our PDLC memory question set with gold evidence ids (at least 200 questions). Metrics: gold-evidence recall, served tokens, answer correctness (judge not sharing the answer model), total cost per correct answer including reranker. Predeclared margin: B or C must keep recall within 1 pp of A and lower cost per correct answer by 10%. Kill: if cost per correct answer is higher than A at every pool size, drop the cutoff for the default path.
why it matters for us: R9 asks whether a System One model earns its place at the reranking stage on our data at a cost we accept; the only field report that counts the reranker's bill says it may not. R3 assembly budgets should be judged on cost per correct answer, not served tokens.
