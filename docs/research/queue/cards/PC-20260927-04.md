claim: On a frozen set of engineering tasks whose artifact pool contains superseded, conflicting and falsely-final documents, serving each retrieved artifact with an authority label (authoritative, superseded, false final, out of scope) and a short evidence report raises task rubric pass rate by at least 5 points over serving the same ranked artifacts unlabelled.
proposed experiment: Arm A = current retrieval, top-k artifacts served as-is; Arm B = same top-k, each passed through a labelling step (one typed choice over the six Env-Rethink authority classes, conditioned on the artifact, its supersession edges and its neighbours) with the label and a one-paragraph evidence report prepended. Dataset: 40 to 60 frozen build-time tasks drawn from our P0 fixtures, each seeded with at least one superseded and one conflicting artifact (versions retained in the ledger). Metric: rubric pass rate per task (predeclared rubric, judge not sharing a model with the agent), secondary tokens served. Predeclared margin: +5 points, paired over tasks, bootstrap 95% interval excluding 0. Kill: margin not reached, or labelling misclassifies an authoritative artifact as superseded on more than 5% of artifacts.
why it matters for us: This tests the R3 claim that task-time curation beats raw serving on exactly the long structured artifacts JITMem did not cover, and it tests the R2 design choice of labelling superseded versions at read rather than hiding them. If it holds, supersession edges become a served signal in the read surface, not only a write-time record.
