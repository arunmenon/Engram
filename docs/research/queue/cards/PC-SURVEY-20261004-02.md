claim: Across 41,203 test issues, a random-forest reranker over the top 20 dense-retrieved commits matched GPT-5.1 at linking an issue to its commit (P@1 0.842 vs 0.822, 0.936 vs 0.920, 0.949 vs 0.953). Open 7B to 8B LLM rerankers were 0.2 to 0.3 worse. A one-year-plus-30-days window kept about 97% of true links (arXiv 2605.00447). An agent that reads issue comments reached Hit@1 0.86 at USD 0.01 and 24 s per issue, and comments carried most of the signal (arXiv 2508.12232). Neither paper decides "no link", and neither tested a System One model.

proposed experiment: On two of our own repositories, mine ground-truth ticket-to-commit links from commit messages that cite ticket keys, then strip the keys. Candidate generation: dense retrieval (all-MiniLM-L12-v2) over commits within ticket creation to closure plus 30 days, top 20, temporal split. Arms:
- (A) random forest on similarity and metadata features (author, time gap, file overlap) trained on the mined links;
- (B) Jev scoring each (ticket, commit) pair as a `noul` "this commit implements this ticket", with ticket comments in the state;
- (C) JevK5-9B self-hosted, same request;
- (D) a frontier LLM listwise reranker with the EasyLink prompt.
Metrics: P@1 and MRR@20; precision of written links at a held-out threshold that also allows "no link" (include 20% of tickets whose true commit is outside the candidate set); cost and p95 latency per ticket. Kill for B: P@1 more than 0.03 below A, or no-link precision below 0.9 at recall 0.7.

why it matters for us: H7 tests traversal over a typed graph whose IMPLEMENTS edges are assumed correct. This test decides who writes those edges and with what confidence, using the first PDLC trace link that has free ground truth. It also tests priority 1 at the linking stage, where the field has no System One evidence.
