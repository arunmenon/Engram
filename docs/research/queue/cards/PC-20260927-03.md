claim: When several candidates are relevant to a read, per-candidate yes/no questions asked in one decision-model request rank relevant evidence higher (nDCG@10) than a single Choice over the same pool, while the Choice shape is at least as good when exactly one candidate is relevant.
proposed experiment: Arm A = single Choice over the fused candidate pool (current B10 design); Arm B = one Noul per candidate in the same request, ranked by probability. Same first-stage candidates (RRF k=60 fusion, top-30) for both arms. Dataset: our P0 read queries split by the number of gold-relevant items (1 vs >= 3), plus LoCoMo-200 if available to reconnect with B10. Metrics: nDCG@10 and gold recall@5 per split, cost and p95 latency per query. Predeclared margin: B beats A by >= 0.02 nDCG@10 on the multi-relevant split with a paired bootstrap interval excluding 0, and A is not worse than B by more than 0.01 on the single-relevant split. Kill: if B does not beat A on the multi-relevant split, keep B10's Choice design.
why it matters for us: R4 treats rerankers as candidate implementations of a decision whose shape must be tested; B10 rests on one vendor's LoCoMo run, while an independent pinned benchmark reverses it when many items are relevant, which is the normal case for build-time reads. The answer decides the rerank call shape and whether rerank scores can double as absolute admission scores (B11).
