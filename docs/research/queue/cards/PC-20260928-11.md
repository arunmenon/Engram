claim: On our own served-memory lines with planted instructions, a Jev injection gate blocks at least 85% of planted lines at a false-block rate at or below 5%, and a self-hosted open-weight detector does not match it.
proposed experiment: Frozen memory store and serving path. Arms: (A) the jevmem-style single injection question at threshold 0.5; (B) the hermes-firewall two-question Jev policy as pinned at 7c5a326; (C) SemIf Qwen3.5-4B self-hosted; (D) no gate, framing only ("facts, not instructions"). Dataset: at least 150 benign memory lines sampled from our store plus at least 150 planted lines written by a person who has not seen the detectors, including instructions phrased as ordinary process (the two jevmem misses) and instructions inside quoted emails (where Laya and DeBERTa were near chance). Metrics: block rate on planted lines, false-block rate on benign lines with the rule-of-three bound (B21), cost per 1,000 lines, latency. Predeclared margin: adopt the cheapest arm whose block rate is at least 85% and whose false-block upper bound is at most 5%. Kill: no arm meets both; then the gate stays advisory and permissions carry the boundary (B25). The detector never becomes the security boundary; this card measures a filter, not authority.
why it matters for us: B24 rests on 44 author-written lines; this public 718-item result is the first larger check and shows a nonzero false-block rate (3.5%) that matters for build-time reads. The test settles which detector sits at the safety stage and whether a self-hosted option is good enough to keep memory text in-house (R8, R9).
