claim: Re-sending byte-identical write-gate and rerank requests to hosted Jev changes the gated outcome on at least 1% of our decisions, while a pinned deterministic self-hosted model changes none, at no more than 3 points lower accuracy.
proposed experiment: Arm A: hosted Jev 1.13, each request sent 5 times; arm B: djev (Bespoke-Nimble-9B on SGLang with deterministic kernels, one H100), each request sent 5 times. Dataset: our frozen write-gate and rerank decision sets (at least 500 requests), thresholds from the harness. Metrics: outcome flip rate across repeats at the production threshold (primary), probability standard deviation, accuracy against human labels, p50 latency. Margin: A's flip rate at least 1 point with B at 0, and B's accuracy no more than 3 points below A. Kill: A's flip rate below 0.5% (re-execution is good enough; store probabilities only for audit) or B more than 5 points less accurate (determinism not worth the quality). Coordinate with PC-SURVEY-20260927-01 (decision replay determinism) so the two measure the same flip-rate definition.
why it matters for us: R1 needs a replay guarantee for every served decision and B1 assumes retaining served artifacts suffices; djev measured hosted Jev returning 15 distinct outputs in 15 identical calls, with one task's answer splitting 6 yes and 9 no, which would make re-execution an invalid receipt. Measuring the flip rate on our own gates decides the receipt format before the write gate ships.
