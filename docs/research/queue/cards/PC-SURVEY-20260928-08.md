claim: On nine generic entity-matching datasets, a 124M matcher reached 81.96 F1 against 86.36 for GPT-4, at "3,899 times lower" cost per 1K tokens. Cross-encoders beat bi-encoders, and generative matchers helped mainly under distribution shift. G13 today specifies a Jev Noul `same_claim` with a rule fallback, but no one has compared it with a small matcher on cross-tool engineering records.

proposed experiment:
- **Cases.** Label 400 record pairs drawn from Jira, GitHub issues and PRs, Confluence requirement pages and CI test records: 160 same item, 240 different but similar. Two labellers, blind. Hold out one whole source pairing (for example Confluence to GitHub) as the shift set.
- **Arms.**
  - A: rules (shared key such as a ticket id in a PR title or URL, exact title).
  - B: an open-weight cross-encoder fine-tuned on the non-held-out pairs.
  - C: a Jev Noul `same_item` with thresholds fit on half.
  - D: a frontier LLM, zero-shot.
- **Metrics.** F1 on the in-distribution and shift sets, cost per 1K pairs, and p95 latency.
- **Kill criterion.**
  - B and C both fail to beat A by 5 F1: ship rules only.
  - C fails to beat B by 3 F1 on either set: ship the open-weight model, which keeps memory text in-house.
  - D fails to beat the best of B and C by 5 F1 on the shift set: no escalation path.

why it matters for us: Spotting the same item across tools is the federation layer's identity problem. The evidence says this is where a small model we host is most likely to be good enough, which serves priority 2 as well.
