claim: Per-question isotonic calibration of Jev probabilities, fitted on our own labelled memory decisions, reduces expected calibration error by at least half on held-out decisions without lowering gate F1 by more than 1 point at validated cutoffs.
proposed experiment: Arm A = raw Jev probabilities for the store/skip and still-true questions with the cutoff chosen on validation; arm B = the same saved Jev outputs passed through a per-question isotonic curve fitted on a training split, with its own cutoff chosen on validation. Dataset: 1,500 or more human-labelled memory decisions from our traffic, split by repository into train, validation and test. Metrics: ECE (15 bins), Brier, log loss, F1 and precision at the chosen cutoff, escalation rate at a fixed precision target. Predeclared margin: ECE reduced by 50% or more with a repository-clustered bootstrap interval excluding 0, and F1 within 1.0 point of arm A. Kill: ECE reduction under 25%, or F1 loss over 1.0 point.
why it matters for us: R9 asks whether Jev is the right System One model at the write gate; a threshold that means what it says is a precondition for setting escalation budgets and for comparing Jev with open-weight models fairly. The TRIVIA+ result suggests calibration fixes meaning, not accuracy, and the gate design should know which.
