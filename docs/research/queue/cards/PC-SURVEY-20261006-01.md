claim: Extracting facts without the ontology in the prompt, mapping types by embedding similarity, and repairing only constraint violations gave 96.8% schema-valid triples vs 79.4% with constraints in the prompt, at 0.59x vs 0.79x relative token cost, with equal QA (OAK+MEND, Qwen3-30B-A3B, n=1000 HotpotQA). A separate study shows small generative models emitting typed links against a schema fail badly (Mistral 7B triple F1 4.2, 41 schema violations on 10 documents).

proposed experiment: On the PDLC slice (requirements, design sections, tickets, pull requests, tests), three arms at matched extractor model: (A) schema in prompt; (B) free extraction, embedding canonicalization, frontier-LLM repair of violations; (C) as B but Jev or an Apache-2.0 open decision model (vllm-sr Decision-2.0-Nox-4B) decides the ambiguous canonicalizations (top candidates within 0.05 cosine) and repairs as yes/no per candidate link type. Measure link precision and recall against a hand-adjudicated gold (adjudicate "false positives" since gold will be incomplete), schema validity, tokens and latency per artifact. Refines H5 (independent typed gate on extraction) and H7 (typed supersession on PDLC questions) by fixing where the schema is enforced.

why it matters for us: Priority 3 says the narrow PDLC slice is to be tested with a System One model deciding links. This tells us where that model should sit (the ambiguity band and the repair step, not free generation) and whether we can drop the schema from every prompt.
