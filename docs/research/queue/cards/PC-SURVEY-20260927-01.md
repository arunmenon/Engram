claim: Retaining the served evidence does not let a reviewer re-derive the decision by re-running the model. At T=0 on identical cases, frontier models reached the same final decision in only 64 to 94 % of replays on the two valid DFAH benchmarks, and tool-signature identity was 20 to 70 %. On stock serving engines two bit-identical batches disagreed on 9 to 23 % of estimates; batch-invariant kernels removed this (0.0 %, n = 106).

proposed experiment: Refines P0 (step 3, replay) and H3's "served briefing replayed byte-identical" kill criterion. P0 checks that the served artifact comes back unchanged. It does not check whether the decision made from that artifact can be re-derived. Add one fixture with three arms on the same 50 Spec-to-PR-review decisions, each with its pinned served briefing: (A) re-run on the hosted API at T=0, 3 replays; (B) re-run on a self-hosted open model with batch-invariant kernels, 3 replays; (C) return the recorded output from the receipt. Metric: decision unanimity per case (DFAH DecDet) and tool-signature unanimity. Predeclare: if A's unanimity is under 95 %, the contract promises only C (artifact plus recorded output), and the receipt schema adds output, model id, provider and sampling parameters as required fields.

why it matters for us: B1 says the retained artifacts are sufficient. That is true for "show what was served" and false for "reproduce the decision" on hosted inference. The replay guarantee Engram sells has to name which one it gives, and the receipt schema follows from that choice. The fixture takes about a day on the P0 harness.
