claim: On automated decision gates, a logistic regression on small sentence embeddings, trained on the task's own labels, beat Jev on intents (0.948 vs 0.913 on CLINC-150, better calibrated as shipped) and was not significantly different on workflow decisions (0.702 vs 0.732). A cheap first stage escalating to Jev matched Jev at 0.43 of its cost (arXiv 2610.00346). Meanwhile the best permissive open model, JevK5-9B, now runs on one 19 GB GPU (N-SURVEY-20261004-01). Fine-tuning an open model must use LoRA, or general decision quality falls even as own-task loss improves (arXiv 2610.02076).

proposed experiment: Extend H5 from "Jev vs the extractor's self-reported confidence" to five arms on the same labelled extracted items, split by project and time into fit, calibration and test sets:
- (A) the extractor's self-reported confidence (H5's baseline);
- (B) hosted Jev (H5's treatment);
- (C) logistic regression on embeddings trained on our labels, with a learning curve at 50, 100, 200 and 400 labels;
- (D) JevK5-9B self-hosted, zero-shot, through the same `/v1/systemone` request;
- (E) cascade of C then B, escalating below a held-out confidence threshold.
Every arm gets an explicit "do not store" option and thresholds fit on held-out data only. Metrics: precision and recall of accepted items, ECE, p95 latency, cost per 1,000 decisions, and whether memory text leaves our network. H5 needs about 90 items; arm C's learning curve needs at least 400 labelled items. Kill for C: below B by more than 3 points F1 at 400 labels. Kill for D: below B by more than 5 points F1, or ECE above 0.15.

why it matters for us: The R9 thesis says a System One model belongs at a stage only where it beats the alternative. The cheapest alternative, a classifier on our own labels, is not in H5 today. If C or E matches B, the write gate keeps memory text in-house (priority 2) at almost no cost. If D matches B, we have a self-hosted fallback for vendor or egress risk.
