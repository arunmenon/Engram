claim: Averaging a single-call choice rerank over 8 random candidate orderings raises recall@1 by at least 2 points over the same call with candidates in RRF order, on our rerank set.
proposed experiment: Arm A = one Jev choice call over the RRF-ordered pool (current design, B10); Arm A2 = same call with the pool in one random order (order-sensitivity control); Arm B = mean choice probability over 8 random orderings batched in one or more calls. Candidate labels are opaque ids in all arms. Dataset: LoCoMo-200 rerank pools used for B10 plus 100 build-time read queries from P0 fixtures, 30 candidates each. Metric: recall@1 and recall@5, plus the rank correlation between candidate position and chosen probability. Predeclared margin: Arm B >= Arm A + 2 points recall@1 on both sets. Kill: Arm A and A2 within 1 point (no order sensitivity) or Arm B gain < 1 point at 8x decision cost.
why it matters for us: R4 asks that a decision be automated only when its error behaviour is understood; our rerank rests on one choice call whose order sensitivity nobody has measured. A positive result changes the rerank call shape; a null result closes a confound on B10.
