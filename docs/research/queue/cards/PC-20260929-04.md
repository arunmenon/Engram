claim: On our own labelled write-gate and linking decisions, at least one open-weight decision model run on our hardware is non-inferior to hosted Jev 1.13 within 3 accuracy points per decision family after local recalibration.
proposed experiment: Arms: hosted Jev 1.13 (pinned); Winnow-12B Q8 or Cygnet (JevBench v1.5 joint leaders, one workstation GPU); Jeff-Qwen3.5-2B (local, Jev-shaped API); optional JEV-27B if an 80 GB-class GPU is available. All arms get identical state and questions through the same /v1/systemone request shape; states over 1,024 tokens reported separately because two arms truncate. Dataset: frozen, human-labelled decisions from our harness: write-gate save/skip and kind, and linking decisions (supersedes, contradicts, and the PDLC trace links implements, verifies, refines), at least 150 per family. Metrics: accuracy per family (primary), ECE after a local temperature refit on a held-out half, p50 latency on our hardware, $ per 1,000 decisions. Margin: paired-bootstrap non-inferiority of 3 points against Jev per family (JevControl-style). Kill: an arm is dropped if it is more than 5 points below Jev on linking or its ECE stays above 0.08 after refit; if every open arm is dropped, linking stays hosted this quarter.
why it matters for us: This is the CTO's priority 2 comparison, on our decisions rather than a public board: R9 asks whether an open model belongs at each memory stage at a cost and privacy we accept. Jeff's own panel shows sub-2B models trailing Jev by 15 to 30 points on reasoning-heavy decisions, so the answer may differ between the write gate and linking, which is exactly what the per-family margin separates.
