claim: On our frozen PDLC memory question set, our hybrid retrieval arm's evidence-hit at k=5 and k=10 exceeds the random-selection expectation at the same k by at least 15 points in every question category, and end-to-end answer accuracy with retrieval is within 5 points of the full-context arm on projects small enough to fit in context.
proposed experiment: Arm A = current hybrid retrieval at k = 3, 5, 10, 20; Arm B = uniform random selection of k memory units from the same project store (computed analytically and by 5 seeded draws); Arm C = full context where the project fits the reader window. Dataset: frozen PDLC benchmark questions with gold evidence ids (held-out split, prompts frozen and hashed before scoring), plus a set of unanswerable questions; abstention offered on every question in every arm. Metrics: evidence-hit observed minus random per category with Wilson 95% intervals; answer accuracy per arm under a code grader where possible and a judge of a different model family otherwise; correct-refusal and false-refusal rates; count of distinct ordered result sets (degeneracy). Predeclared margin: retrieval minus random >= 15 points at k=5 in every category. Kill: if any category has retrieval minus random < 5 points at k=5, or false refusal > 10%, the retrieval arm is not reported as a lift and the category goes to a retrieval fix before any other read-path experiment.
why it matters for us: R6 needs every read-path number to sit beside the controls that make it readable, and Caura's harness shows a vendor retriever indistinguishable from random while the full-context line scored 87%. R3 decisions about graph traversal, reranking and stop rules are meaningless until the base retriever is shown to beat random selection on our own engineering stores.
