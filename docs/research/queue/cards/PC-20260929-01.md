claim: On our own labelled Jev choice decisions, thresholding on the probability of the chosen option gives lower calibration error and at least equal automation coverage at a fixed error rate than thresholding on the API's `confidence` field.
proposed experiment: Arm A gates on the top-class option probability; arm B gates on the `confidence` field (the peak statistic (K*p_max-1)/(K-1)); arm C is arm A after a local temperature or isotonic refit on a held-out half. Dataset: at least 500 logged, human-labelled Jev choice decisions from our frozen harness across write-gate kind, recall/no-recall and routing, with an ambiguous-input slice reported separately; Jev version pinned. Metrics: calibration error with equal-mass bins (bin count fixed in advance) and coverage at 2% error; noise floor from 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Predeclared margin: arm A beats arm B by at least 0.03 calibration error with coverage no worse; arm C is adopted only if it beats A by at least 0.02. Kill: A vs B difference inside the bootstrap noise floor, in which case keep either field and record the null.
why it matters for us: This is an R9 question about how a System One model is wired at every memory stage: one builder measured 0.12 vs 0.19 and the RSI-Jev v3.0 release documents that `confidence` is not a probability, but nobody has measured it on memory decisions. Settling it once fixes the input to every threshold under B14 before the write-gate and recall-gate experiments consume it.
