claim: ProReFiCIA finds 85.7% of requirements impacted by a change while an analyst reviews 3.0% of the document (95.8% at 3.4% with RAG), against 35.1% for RAG plus chain-of-thought and 99% review cost for an embedding threshold at similar recall. Tested on 11 change rationales in one document.

proposed experiment: From one real project, collect 20 to 30 historical requirement changes with the requirements, design sections and tests they actually affected (from later edits and PRs). Arms: (a) embedding similarity with a tuned cutoff; (b) one-prompt LLM proposal plus refinement, no filter; (c) (b) plus NLI filter as in the paper; (d) (b) plus a System One filter (Jev or the winner of PC-SURVEY-20261011-01) asked "is this item affected by the change?". Metrics: recall of affected items and share of items a person must review. H7 tests supersession and completeness questions over a typed graph; it does not test finding impacted items from a change, so this adds a question class to H7's seeded project rather than duplicating it.

why it matters for us: "Notice a changed requirement" is one of the five tasks the CTO named for our PDLC memory benchmark, and the filter slot is a natural first System One stage in the PDLC slice.
