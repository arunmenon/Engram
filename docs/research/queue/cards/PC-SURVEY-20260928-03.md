claim: RRF with k=60 is sensitive to k and was beaten on every reported dataset (MS MARCO, NQ, Quora, NFCorpus, HotpotQA, Fever) by a normalised convex combination (for example MS MARCO NDCG@1000 0.425 vs 0.454), with alpha converging on under 5 % of training queries (arXiv 2210.11934).

proposed experiment: Refines H2, whose arms differ only in graph traversal and hold the fusion function fixed. Add a fusion sub-study on H2's harness and budget: (A) RRF k=60 shared; (B) RRF with k tuned per channel; (C) convex combination with per-channel min-max or theoretical min-max normalisation, alpha (or per-channel weights) fitted on a small labelled split and frozen. Graph on in all three. Metrics: accuracy and token-F1 at equal retrieved-token budget, with H2's noise floor. Kill for C: no gain over A beyond the noise floor, or no stable weights because a channel lacks a usable score.

why it matters for us: If fusion alone moves accuracy by a margin comparable to the graph lift H2 is looking for, H2's verdict on the graph depends on an untested fusion choice. It costs one extra sweep on an existing harness.
