claim: On investigation questions about our own codebase, a coding agent with Jev-filtered code search (genigrep at da10c5e, lexical guard on) spends less and finishes faster than the plain grep-and-read loop at equal blind-graded answer quality, and beats keyword-only ranking on quality.
proposed experiment: Frozen coding agent and model. Arms: (A) plain ripgrep and file-read loop; (B) genigrep with Jev filtering (file threshold 0.6, passage 0.5, definition following up to 6, top-5 keyword guard, as pinned); (C) genigrep with keyword-only ranking (no Jev). Dataset: at least 40 real investigation questions about our repositories written before the run, plus 20 bug-fix tasks with executable tests. Metrics: answer quality graded blind by two graders from a model family other than the agent's, with a human-checked subsample; dollar cost and wall time per question; pass rate on bug-fix tasks; paired bootstrap intervals; 2 runs per arm. Predeclared margin: B adopted if its cost is at least 5% lower than A with the interval excluding zero and answer quality not lower by more than 2 points. Kill: B's quality below A's by more than 2 points, or no cost or time saving with the interval excluding zero. Also log, as the note found, whether wording that tells the agent results are "sufficient" makes it stop early (the B9 stop-rule risk).
why it matters for us: R9 asks whether a System One model earns its place at the reranking stage on our workload; this is the first coding-agent report with intervals, and it shows a smaller saving than jevgrep's and none on bug fixing. The test decides whether code search with Jev enters our agent toolset and whether stop-early wording is a hazard for any sufficiency signal we add (R3).
