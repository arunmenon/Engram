claim: On our own recorded write-gate and recall-gate decisions, a frozen-encoder student fitted to Jev's distributions with a 95%-confidence Clopper-Pearson threshold answers at least 50% of calls locally while disagreeing with Jev on no more than 2% of all calls.
proposed experiment: Arm A always calls Jev; arm B is a Jevstiller-style cascade (bge-small plus logistic head on Jev soft labels, kNN OOD gate, bound-chosen threshold at target 98%, 2% audit) and arm C the same cascade with a point-estimate threshold, all replayed on a frozen log of at least 5,000 of our Jev gate decisions with 2,000 held out over 20 seeded splits. Metrics: held-out local coverage, disagreement with Jev over all calls (mean, worst, violations of 2%), and downstream recall@5 on our memory eval set. Predeclared margin: arm B coverage >= 50% with <= 1/20 split violations. Kill: coverage < 30% or downstream recall@5 drops more than 1 point vs arm A. Multi-question calls (nouls plus choice) need one student per question; report each.
why it matters for us: R9 asks whether a System One model can sit at a memory stage at a cost and privacy we accept; a distilled student with a measurable agreement contract is a cheap path to keeping memory text in-house. It also tests R4's claim that local, bounded thresholds beat point estimates and vendor confidence.
