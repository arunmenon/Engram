claim: Without any training, an iterative read loop (open, search, browse, stop when the evidence is sufficient, at most 20 rounds) over raw source records answers more of our read queries correctly than one-shot hybrid retrieval over the same records at the same online token budget.
proposed experiment: arm A one-shot hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus dense, reciprocal-rank fusion k=60, top-k sized to the token budget) vs arm B the iterative loop with a sufficiency stop and a 20-round cap, same frozen backbone, same raw store, no fine-tuning in either arm · dataset: the P0 read queries with gold evidence labels, plus a LongMemEval-S subset as an anchor to the paper · metric: answer accuracy and gold-evidence recall, with online tokens and latency per query reported for both arms · predeclared margin: arm B at least 5 accuracy points above arm A with online tokens at or below 1.0x of arm A · kill: arm B under 2 points better, or more than 2x the latency of arm A, in which case the loop is not built this quarter. Design warning from the source: the paper's untrained loop scored 48.80 on LongMemEval against 56.20 for one-shot RAG, so the prior is that the untrained loop loses on chat memory.
why it matters for us: R3 asks us to select sufficient authorised evidence within a bounded budget; this test says whether a multi-step read with a stop rule earns its latency on our build-time reads. It also gives B9 (a sufficiency stop can cut tokens at equal accuracy) its first measurement on our own queries.
