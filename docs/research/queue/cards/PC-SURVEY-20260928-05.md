claim: On jev-1.13.0, appending one unverified opinion to the state flipped 12.1% of 812 typed decisions (+11.1 pp over a 1.0% re-run floor), tied with the strongest injected command, and pushed 38.0% of confident (>= 0.8) answers below 0.8 versus 0.5% on re-run (arXiv 2609.31142). Optimised fluent context flipped 61.4% of correct Jev decisions and 64.9% to 73.2% for open-weight decision systems (arXiv 2609.30243). Command-style injection rarely hijacks Jev (1.8%, 3.5% adaptive; arXiv 2609.28613). No paper tests a defence.

proposed experiment: Refines H10 (a) staleness audit and (b) injection gate, which plan to feed memory text to Jev but do not measure contamination by benign-looking opinions. Take 200 real link or staleness decisions from project history (Decision pairs: does B supersede A? is A still true?). Build the state three ways, same question and options: (A) target memories only; (B) target memories plus their retrieved neighbours as free text, as the serving path would; (C) as B, but neighbours placed in a separate field labelled by `source_class` (user-stated, tool-result, inferred) and with opinion sentences marked unverified. Add a probe set where one neighbour carries a planted unverified opinion contradicting the gold answer. Label-free scoring per JevAdvBench: compare each decision with arm A and with an identical re-run of A. Metrics: flip rate over floor, share of >= 0.8 answers crossing the threshold, and F1 on the 100 items with gold labels. Kill for C: flip rate not lower than B by more than the re-run floor. Kill for passing neighbours at all: B flips more than 5 pp over A on the non-probe set.

why it matters for us: Our memories are full of people's opinions about decisions. If passing them to Jev as context moves one decision in eight, automatic linking and staleness flags will drift with whatever was said most recently, and the 0.8 review threshold will not catch it. This costs a few hundred calls and tells us how to build every state before those gates ship.
