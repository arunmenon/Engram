claim: On CL-Bench, naive in-context history had the highest normalised gain (25.4 % Sonnet 4.6). With GPT-5.4 held fixed it gained 20.1 %, vs 8.6 % for an ACE playbook (at 3.4x the cost) and 7.8 % for agent-kept notes; Mem0 matched it (20.2 %). Model-authored skills scored 8.1 to 11.5 pp below no skills on SkillsBench.

proposed experiment: Refines H3 (and borrows H1's harness). H3's three arms are all derived (session summary, Workflow node text, curated briefing from top-3 raw episodes); none is raw history. Add arm 4, raw prior-session history in context up to the executor's window, and arm 0, stateless. Run on CL-Bench Codebase Adaptation (tablib 9 issues, tenacity 10 issues; reward 1 - (steps-1)/max_steps) plus the DreamBench-SWE sequences H1 already uses. Report the CL-Bench gain (stateful minus own-stateless). Predeclare: a derived arm ships as a read surface only if it beats arm 4 by a margin at equal or lower tokens. Where history exceeds the window, arm 4 becomes truncated most-recent history.

why it matters for us: If raw history in the window beats curated briefings, Engram's value is retrieval once history no longer fits, not curation. That changes what Consumer 4 is for. CL-Bench's SE sequences are small and executable, so the added arm is cheap.
