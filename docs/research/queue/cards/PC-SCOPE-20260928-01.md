claim: No public memory benchmark grades the questions a product-development memory must answer (decision reasons weeks later, superseded requirements, PR-to-requirement links, repeated failed fixes, stale memories, abstention) on real engineering history. LoCoMo is near saturation with a 6.4 % wrong answer key and a lenient grader; LongMemEval self-reports cluster at 89 to 95 %; DolphinBench grades office actions on synthetic personas; CL-Bench has 19 bug-fix issues and no requirements; DreamBench-SWE covers multi-session coding only. A benchmark of our own is the only way to test the PDLC layer's claims. This is a design opinion; nothing is measured yet.

proposed experiment: Build v1 of the PDLC memory benchmark and run it once.
- Task families (6): F1 decision and its reason weeks later; F2 requirement changed, older statements superseded; F3 link a PR to the requirement it implements, with a planted plausible wrong candidate; F4 warn before repeating a failed fix; F5 spot a stale memory after the code under it changed; F6 abstain when memory has nothing.
- Items: 60 scored items per family (360) plus 120 benign controls for F4 and F5, 480 in total; half from our own repository, issues, PRs, design docs and session transcripts (private split), half from public spec-driven repositories in SpecMine's Kiro and PR layers (published split). Gold drafted from artifact structure (closing keywords, typed references, reverts, content hashes), verified by two annotators with 30 % double labelling; every item must pass with evidence and fail without history (DolphinBench validity filter).
- Systems: Engram (ledger-only and graph), Mem0 OSS, Zep (Graphiti OSS), Letta, Hindsight, supermemory if its terms allow, a no-memory reader and a full-context reader; same reader model throughout, three runs each, cost and p50/p95 latency per query. Then swap the decider at one memory stage at a time (linking, recall gate, staleness, pre-action warning) between an LLM, Jev and an open-weight System One model.
- Useful if: system rankings on our items differ materially from rankings on LongMemEval and DolphinBench for the same systems, or at least two families separate systems by more than the noise floor with paired intervals excluding zero, and at least one stage swap gives a decision we would act on.
- Kill: if system rankings on our items agree with public-benchmark rankings (Kendall tau at least 0.8 over the systems run on both) and no family separates systems beyond the noise floor, ours adds nothing and we stop at v1 and use the public ones. Also stop and redesign if the validity filter rejects more than half of any family's items (the questions are answerable without memory).
- Size of effort: about 8 weeks of one engineer after the eval-harness fixes, about 160 annotator hours, roughly USD 3,000 to 8,000 of model spend (estimate), most of it on the full-context rows.

why it matters for us: Priority 4 in the CTO's list. Every PDLC-layer decision in the discovery plan (typed supersession, trace links, staleness audits, the pre-action gate, where Jev sits) is currently judged on chat-memory tests that do not contain these questions, and our own harness leaked labels. Without this benchmark we cannot say to a customer that Engram helps their engineers more than Mem0 or Zep, and we cannot tell which memory stages are worth a System One model. If the kill criterion fires, we save the effort of maintaining a benchmark and lean on public ones.
