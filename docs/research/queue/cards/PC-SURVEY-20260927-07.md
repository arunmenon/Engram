claim: A coding agent (SE-Agent, Qwen3-Coder-480B) built memory from 3 rounds of benign SWE-Bench-verified work. Afterwards its RedCode-Gen refusal rate fell from 99.4% to 54.4% and ASR rose from 0.6% to 20.6%. No attacker was involved. A one-line "memories are references, not rules" framing recovered part of this: ASR 13.1%, refusal 66.9%. In skill evolution, all 21 evolved configurations authored unsafe artifacts. A write-time critic plus retirement (SafeEvolve) cut unsafe retrieval from 35.33% to 8.67% and clean-session harm from 21.33% to 4.00%. Benign task success was flat (58.44 to 58.00), but benign success on clean-session probes fell from 53.33 to 40.67.

proposed experiment: This refines the governance check "track guardrail-trigger rate across rounds" and H10(b). It is not covered by H4 or H10 as written: H10 gates planted injections, whereas here the memory is benign and self-generated. Build Engram memory from 3 rounds of benign coding tasks, then probe with a fixed set of at least 150 independent unsafe-request probes and at least 150 benign probes (enough for H10's 2% false-block bound).
- Arm A: raw serving.
- Arm B: serving with the reference-not-rule framing.
- Arm C: framing plus a write-time critic that may only delete or narrow procedure text.
- Control: no memory.
Metrics:
- Refusal and guardrail-trigger rate relative to the no-memory control.
- Unsafe retrieval rate (URR, the share of probe tasks that retrieve a flagged item).
- Benign task success on held-out coding tasks.
- Added tokens.
Judges must differ from the agent model.

why it matters for us: B18 moves from essay-grade support to measured support in a coding agent. The drift appears with no adversary, so Engram's own benign memory can be the cause. If Arm A shows a drop in refusal or guardrail triggers against the no-memory control, framing or write-admission becomes a shipping requirement, not an option.
