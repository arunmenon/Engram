claim: On our own write-gate and linking decisions, local Laya served through Unsloth's Jev-compatible API, with thresholds refit on its `probabilities` output, reaches within 3 points of hosted Jev on precision at the automation threshold at no more than 2x its p50 latency on a CPU host.
proposed experiment: Arm A = hosted Jev (jev-latest) through the TypeSafe SDK with our current thresholds. Arm B = the same SDK with TYPESAFE_BASE_URL pointed at Unsloth's local /v1/systemone serving Laya (English typed-decisions variant for 512-token units, Multilingual for up to 1024), thresholds refit on `probabilities` (not `confidence`, whose formula differs) on a dev split. Identical questions (noul, choice with at most 20 described options, score). Dataset: 300 labelled PDLC decisions (150 write-gate save or skip, 150 link or supersede decisions between requirement, design and ticket units), each under 1024 tokens, split 100 dev and 200 test. Metric: precision and coverage at the automation threshold, macro accuracy, p50 and p95 latency, 400 and 422 rejection rate. Predeclared margin: Arm B precision within 3 points of Arm A at equal or higher coverage. Kill: if Arm B is more than 5 points worse at matched coverage, or more than 5% of real units exceed the context window, keep hosted Jev and record the gap.
why it matters for us: Priority 2 is running a decision model ourselves so memory text stays in-house; this is the cheapest possible test because the swap is a base URL change on the SDK we already use. It measures R9 quality at the two stages where the field has no evidence, and records the compatibility gaps (confidence formula, option trimming, context limit) before anyone builds on them.
