claim: On our frozen engineering-agent traces, a decision-model pass/fail judge is no worse than our LLM judge on agreement with blinded human labels, has higher failure recall, and flips fewer verdicts across repeated calls, at lower cost per decision.
proposed experiment: Arm A = current LLM judge (structured verdict + confidence); Arm B = decision-model judge with one binary rubric proposition per trace and a threshold fixed before the run. Dataset: a frozen set of at least 300 of our harness traces, with a blinded human-labelled audit subset sized from B21 (at least 150 traces including at least 50 failures), plus 100 traces x 20 repeats for repeatability. Metrics: human agreement, failure recall on labelled failures, count of traces with verdict flips, cost and p50 latency per decision. Predeclared margin: B within 5 points of A on human agreement (paired bootstrap lower bound >= -5), B failure recall >= A, and B flips <= A. Kill: if B's failure recall is lower than A's by more than 5 points or the agreement lower bound is below -5, keep the LLM judge.
why it matters for us: R6 requires traceable protocols and uncertainty; a judge that is cheap and repeatable enough to run on every change changes how often we can measure, but only if it does not miss failures. The neosigma study (25 vs 17 of 29 failures caught, 3 vs 19 flipped traces) is one team on customer-service traces; our long engineering traces may behave differently.
