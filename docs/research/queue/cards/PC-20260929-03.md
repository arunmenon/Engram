claim: On labelled engineering-agent turns, write-gate thresholds fitted on our own labels beat both published fixed configurations (jevmem: content >= 0.5, importance >= useful, chit-chat < 0.5; AI Maestro: durable >= 0.75, importance >= 2.5 of 4, correction bypass at durable >= 0.5) on save/skip F1 by at least 3 points.
proposed experiment: Same Jev version and the same batched questions (durable, category with none, importance, correction, chit-chat, injection) answered once per turn and cached, so arms differ only in the decision rule. Arm A: jevmem rule; arm B: AI Maestro rule including the correction bypass; arm C: thresholds fitted by cross-validation on half the labels (on option probabilities, see PC-20260929-01). Dataset: at least 150 frozen, human-labelled coding, review and incident turns (save or skip, and kind), including at least 30 corrections of an earlier decision. Metrics: save/skip F1 (primary), recall on corrections, false saves of chit-chat or injected text. Margin: C beats both A and B by at least 3 F1 points on the held-out half. Kill: C does not beat both, in which case adopt the better fixed rule and stop tuning.
why it matters for us: The write gate is the R9 decision the CTO ranked first to test, and two builders ship different untested thresholds for it; B14 says thresholds must be fitted locally, and this card measures whether fitting buys anything. With 150 cases, zero false saves on injected lines would bound that rate near 2% (B21), enough to set the gate default for the PDLC memory.
