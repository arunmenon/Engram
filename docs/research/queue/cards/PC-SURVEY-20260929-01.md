claim: In a pre-registered test (arXiv 2609.34227), storing raw conversation turns and asking Jev one yes/no relevance question per shortlisted turn was non-inferior to gpt-4o-mini fact extraction on LoCoMo at about 250 served tokens (77.0% vs 77.5%, one-sided bound -3.0 against a -5 margin; human strict -1.7, bound -3.9), at $0.0006 vs $1.865 write cost per 1,000 turns and 273 ms vs 816 ms read p50 against an LLM reranker. At 20 served items extraction won (82.4 vs 77.6). The rerank dropped all evidence for 10.4% of questions and lowered correct abstention (54.1 vs 63.6). All results are on chat memory with a gpt-4o-mini judge that shares the answer model.

proposed experiment: Refines H3 (read-time curation vs write-time summaries) and H1 (raw ledger floor) by adding the arm this paper tested, on engineering history instead of chat. Data: the H1 DreamBench-SWE sequences plus 150 PDLC questions from the scoped PDLC benchmark (decision behind a design, changed requirement, which PR implements what). Arms at two predeclared budgets (about 300 and about 1,500 served tokens): (A) raw ledger events, BM25+embedding shortlist of 30, no selector; (B) A plus one Jev Noul per shortlisted event with the paper's option wording, top k by probability; (C) A plus the same Noul on a self-hosted open-weight model (Jev-Style-2B or Laya) and a label log-probability arm on an open backbone, per the audit checklist in N-SURVEY-20260929-05; (D) our current LLM extraction at write time, retrieved the same way. Recall/no-recall gate held fixed and separate in every arm. Metrics: oracle pass count or exact-answer accuracy with a judge from a different model family than the answerer; write cost per 1,000 events; read p50 and p95; evidence-dropped rate; abstention on unanswerable questions. Kill for replacing extraction: B more than 3 points below D at the small budget, or B's evidence-dropped rate above 10% on PDLC questions.

why it matters for us: Paying a large model to summarise everything we store is the biggest running cost of memory and sends customer text to an outside model. One careful test says a cheap decision model can pick the right raw notes at answer time just as well, for a tiny fraction of the cost, when little context is handed over. If that holds on engineering work, we can drop most write-time extraction, keep exact original text for audits, and run the picker ourselves.
