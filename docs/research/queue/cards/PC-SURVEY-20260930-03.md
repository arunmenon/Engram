claim: On our replayed task stream, the gain of a learn-from-outcomes memory arm over memory off measured in the original task order shrinks by at least half, or changes sign, when the same stream is replayed in two random orders; and the memory arm's run-to-run standard deviation is larger than the memory-off arm's. Source result (N-SURVEY-20260930-05): ReasoningBank +1.5 points in default order (p=0.23 on three runs) and 4.1 to 5.0 points below no memory under two shuffles; variance rose in 17 of 24 cases; best-to-worst gap up to 10.42 points.

proposed experiment: Before H4 and H6 report, run each arm (memory off; access-count reinforcement; outcome-linked reinforcement) for at least 3 full replays under 3 orders: original, and two seeded shuffles that keep session boundaries intact. Report per order the mean, standard deviation and best-to-worst gap, and the paired difference to memory off with an interval clustered by task family. Add one variant of the outcome-linked arm whose lesson writer receives the test results and error text and an explicit scope statement. Kill: the gain in shuffled orders is within 1 point of the gain in the original order and the standard deviations do not differ, in which case order need not be a standing control.

why it matters for us: H4 and H6 both measure learning on a replayed stream, and the stream's order is currently uncontrolled. An independent re-run of two published methods found the headline gain was a product of the default order. Running the shuffles costs a few extra replays on a harness we are already building, and it protects the two experiments that decide whether the loop ships.
