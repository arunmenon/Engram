claim: On our P0 fixture set, replacing the model that writes summaries changes answer accuracy from the summary store by at least 5 points in at least one direction, while the typed-record store moves by less than 1 point; and a half-migrated embedding index recovers less than half of the accuracy gain of a full re-embed. Source result (N-SURVEY-20260930-01): notes moved +9.91 and −13.28 points by direction, fixed-schema graph ±0.1 point, mixed index +4.96 against +11.90 for full re-embed, store-only repair 0/48.

proposed experiment: Extend P0's mutation step with three store-level swaps on the same frozen history and question set. Arms: (1) summary store written by model A, read by A and by B, and the reverse; (2) typed-record store written by A and by B, read by each; (3) embedding index at old version, 50/50 mixed, fully re-embedded, and per-version routed. Repair arm: rebuild the summary store for the new reader with and without access to the raw ledger. Metric: exact or oracle-checked answer accuracy per arm and direction, retained performance after swap, repair success at a 90 % recovery target, repair cost. Kill: all swap deltas under 2 points and mixed index within 2 points of full re-embed, in which case swap testing of the store format is not worth a standing fixture.

why it matters for us: The enterprise contract asks for a swap test. The only controlled swap study found shows the damage depends on store format and on direction, and that the raw history is what makes repair possible. If this holds on our data, summaries become cache behind the ledger, every vector needs an embedder-version field with mixed-version search refused, and fixed-schema nodes gain a portability argument that does not depend on retrieval lift.
