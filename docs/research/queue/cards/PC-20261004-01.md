claim: Fine-tuning an open-weight decision model on teacher labels filtered by teacher self-agreement (at least 85% over two passes) gives higher held-out accuracy on our link decisions than fine-tuning on single-pass teacher labels, at the same training-set size.
proposed experiment: Arm A fine-tunes one open decision model (Laya or Intern-Decision, frozen recipe) on single-pass teacher labels for our PDLC link questions (implements, verifies, supersedes, blocks); arm B fine-tunes the same model on the same items after labelling each twice and dropping question types below 85% self-agreement and items where the two passes disagree, resampling to equal size. Evaluate both on a human-labelled held-out set of at least 300 link decisions, report per-question accuracy, macro F1 and calibration error, against the majority-class baseline and Jev zero-shot. Predeclared margin: arm B beats arm A by at least 5 points macro F1. Kill: margin under 2 points, or neither arm beats the majority-class baseline on a question type (then that question type is cut, not tuned).
why it matters for us: Priority 2 asks us to run our own decision model and tune it on our own decisions (R9); the cheapest failure is training on labels our teacher cannot reproduce. The result decides which R10 link questions we can hand to a System One model at the linking stage and which stay rules or human review.
