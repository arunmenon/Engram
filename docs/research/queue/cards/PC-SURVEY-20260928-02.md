claim: Handoff summaries keep facts and lose binding force and scope. Normal compression produced 100.0 % blocker deactivation and 54.2 % forbidden action; restoring four typed fields (stop status, unresolved prerequisite, responsible authority, admissible fallback) gave 0.0 % forbidden action, while any single field alone did not (arXiv 2608.24569). A 25-word handoff cut boundary-marker survival to 0.56 to 0.69 while fact survival stayed about 0.97 to 0.99; a gold typed allowlist gave 0/48 leaks and a flipped one 44/48 (arXiv 2608.29028).

proposed experiment: Refines H3 (curated briefing) and H9 (summary generation), neither of which has an arm on the briefing schema. Seed about 30 PDLC constraints from a real project (for example "do not merge until security review", "API frozen until v2 sign-off") and about 30 scope-limited items (reviewer-only, tenant-limited). Two arms on the same retrieved evidence and budget: (A) current prose briefing or summary; (B) the same briefing with constraints emitted as typed non-compressible records with the four fields and each item carrying a typed scope field. Executor restricted to the briefing performs the next PDLC step. Metrics: forbidden-action rate, blocker recognition, scope leakage, briefing tokens. Include a corrupted-field fixture (flip scope, drop stop status) to prove the executor is reading the fields. Kill: B not lower than A on forbidden action and leakage beyond noise, or more than 15 % token increase.

why it matters for us: P0 requires "no permission transfer through a snapshot" and B25 requires that evidence not authorise itself; both break silently if summaries turn "must" into "maybe" or drop scope. This is a cheap schema test that can run on H3's harness.
