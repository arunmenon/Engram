claim: On our frozen harness, serving each gate-accepted lesson only within the task family that produced it (falling back to the base policy elsewhere) yields higher held-out task utility and fewer harmful accepted updates than serving the same accepted lessons globally.
proposed experiment: Arm A = current gated promotion with lessons served globally; Arm B = identical proposals and identical gate decisions, lessons stored in family slots and served only to tasks of that family (base policy otherwise). Dataset: a stream of our engineering-agent tasks labelled into 4 to 9 families, ordered in rounds, with a fixed checkpoint bank per family, at least 8 streams with pinned seeds. Metrics: mean trajectory utility on the checkpoint banks and count of harmful accepted updates (accepted update that lowers next-checkpoint utility); also report a no-update static baseline. Predeclared margin: Arm B beats Arm A by >= 0.03 mean utility with a paired bootstrap 95% interval excluding 0, and harmful acceptances in B <= A. Kill: if the interval includes 0 and harmful acceptances do not differ, or if Arm A does not fall below static on any stream (no interference to fix in our workload), drop the scope requirement.
why it matters for us: R2 says updates, supersession and promotion need distinct semantics; this paper shows a held-out gate alone let 6 of 8 harmful global updates through, so scope may be part of promotion semantics rather than an optional tag. If it holds on our tasks, every lesson record needs a scope field and the read path needs scope-aware serving before we build the outcome loop (R5).
