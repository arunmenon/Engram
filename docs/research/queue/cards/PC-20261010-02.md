claim: When stores and views are exposed as explicit named tools, a decision model choosing the tool plus a small LLM writing the query matches a frontier model's end-task success on our memory requests at under a quarter of the cost.
proposed experiment: Arm A frontier LLM native tool calling over our memory tools; arm B Jev picks the tool, small fast LLM writes arguments; arm C open-weight decision model in place of Jev. Same frozen tool set, same turn budget for all arms (WindTunnel's unequal budgets are a known confound). Dataset: at least 150 PDLC memory requests with deterministic outcome checks (right store hit, right ids returned), including hard multi-hop ones so success does not saturate. Metrics: success by majority of 3 attempts, median cost and latency per request. Margin: B within 2 pp of A on success. Kill: B more than 5 pp below A, or A and B both above 98% (benchmark too easy; rebuild before deciding).
why it matters for us: R9 routing is the stage where a decision model choosing among explicit options is most likely to win, and WindTunnel shows it does only when the options are explicit. R7 tool contracts then become the precondition for a cheap router.
