claim: Serving superseded values with explicit validity dates (supersede-and-retain) raises previous-value accuracy by at least 15 points over hiding them (supersede-and-hide) while costing no more than 3 points of current-value accuracy, and the overall accuracy of the two arms differs by less than the previous-value gap (the MemProbe pattern: Graphiti vs LangMem tied overall, p>0.37, previous-value 96.4 vs 67.9, p=0.002).
proposed experiment: Arm A = our store with supersede-and-hide (only the current value is served). Arm B = same store with supersede-and-retain (old and new values served, each with valid-from and valid-to and a supersedes link). Same reader model, same retrieval budget, same extraction. Dataset: MemProbe suite56 through its adapter interface, plus 30 PDLC episodes built with its authoring kit in which a requirement, design decision or constraint changes mid-history. Metric: current-value accuracy, previous-value accuracy and change-detection accuracy reported separately, typed-field scoring against gold, paired bootstrap over episodes with 3 runs per arm. Predeclared margin: previous-value gain at least 15 points with current-value loss at most 3 points. Kill: if Arm B loses more than 3 points on current value, or the previous-value gain is under 5 points, keep hide as default and drop retain.
why it matters for us: R2 asks for distinct semantics for supersession; engineering agents need both "what is the requirement now" and "what did we decide before and why", and B3 cannot be tested with a single accuracy number. This card also fixes a scoring rule for our PDLC memory benchmark (priority 4): report the stability and plasticity sides separately.
