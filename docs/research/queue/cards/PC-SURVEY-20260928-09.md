claim: Palantir and Fabric IQ both type data through a binding made once per source (column to property, identity key), not by tagging each item when it is served. Magneto shows a small retriever with a large-model rerank maps fields to types at 0.860 MRR, against about 0.35 for a classic matcher. LLM-only mapping falls from 0.79 at 23 target classes to 0.51 at 300. Our G7 is written as a Choice per item at serve time.

proposed experiment:
- **Setup.** Take three real backends (Jira, a GitHub repo, a Confluence space) and 300 served items, with a gold type and property for each item's key fields, labelled blind.
- **Arms.**
  - A: serve-time G7 Choice per item (Jev, fixed tag sets).
  - B: bind-time mapping. A small encoder retrieves the top 5 shared properties per source field, a typed Choice picks one, and a person confirms it once. Items are then typed by lookup.
  - C: B, plus serve-time A only for fields left unbound.
- Then change one source's schema (rename two Jira custom fields) and re-run.
- **Metrics.** Type accuracy on served items, serve-time p95 latency and cost per 1K items, and accuracy after the schema change with and without the binding migration rule.
- **Kill criterion.**
  - B or C is more than 3 points of type accuracy below A: keep G7 at serve time.
  - A is within 1 point of C and adds under 50 ms p95: bindings are not worth their upkeep; keep serve-time only.
  - The schema change silently mistypes more than 5% of items under B without a migration flag: the binding needs a breaking-change check before it ships.

why it matters for us: This is the one gateway change the survey recommends (the ontology as a versioned per-backend contract). It needs a measurement before it goes into the design.
