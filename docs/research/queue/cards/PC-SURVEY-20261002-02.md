claim: With faulty memories already identified, dependency-guided rollback recovered 0.853 of 150 controlled GPT-4o cases with 1.000 benign memory kept, vs full reset 0.407 (0.000 kept) and deleting the retrieved memories 0.340 (2608.10502). Recurrence after repair was 0.266, worse than LLM-judge repair 0.121; full affected-set replay cut it to 0.071 at 24.01 LLM calls. Transfer set (50 LongMemEval-V2 cases): 0.680 vs 0.540.
proposed experiment: On H10's seeded project history, log memory-read receipts and produced files/commits per session (P4). Inject 30 bad memories (poisoned, stale, wrong-scope). Arms: delete source only; full reset of the agent's memory; receipt-graph rollback with selective replay. Metrics: task recovery on a re-run, benign memories retained, recurrence over the next 5 sessions, LLM calls. Run with a real detector (H10a staleness audit) as well as oracle diagnosis, since the paper only tests oracle diagnosis.
why it matters for us: R8 lists recovery but no register experiment tests it. If rollback needs receipts we are not recording, P4/P5 are cheaper to extend now than after Consumer 4 ships.
