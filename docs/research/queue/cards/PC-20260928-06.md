claim: An open-weight 0.8B to 4B decision model fine-tuned with soft labels on about 5,000 of our own memory decisions matches zero-shot Jev within 2 points of accuracy on held-out repositories for store/skip and supersedes decisions, at a median latency under 50 ms on one GPU.
proposed experiment: Arm A = Jev (pinned version) zero-shot with our production question wording; arm B = od1-style fine-tune of Qwen3.5-0.8B and 4B (recipe from the od1 card: cross-entropy against soft targets, validation early stop) on decisions from training repositories, labels = averaged human or adjudicated labels. Dataset: 5,000 training and 1,000 test decisions, test drawn only from repositories unseen in training. Metrics: accuracy, Brier, KL to soft labels, p50 and p95 latency for 1, 5 and 20 questions per state, cost per 1,000 decisions. Predeclared margin: B within 2.0 points of A on accuracy (repository-clustered 95% interval of A minus B upper bound under 2.0) and Brier not worse by more than 0.01. Kill: B more than 4 points below A on unseen repositories, or 20-question latency above 150 ms.
why it matters for us: This is the priority-2 goal in R9 terms: keep memory text in-house with a System One model tuned on our own decisions, compared with Jev on our data rather than on a teacher-labelled synthetic benchmark. Testing on unseen repositories guards against the template-overlap effect the od1 card itself flags.
