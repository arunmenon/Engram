claim: On our PDLC memory questions, replacing our hosted extractor with a self-hosted open-weight extractor (about 31B, as in the Acropolis runs) lowers answer quality by no more than 2 points when the answering model and the grader are held fixed.
proposed experiment: Frozen memory pipeline, same corpus, same retrieval and budget, same answering model and grader. Arms: (A) current hosted extractor; (B) self-hosted open-weight extractor of about 31B on our own GPU; (C) all-local: open-weight extractor and open-weight reader, to price the answer seat. Dataset: our PDLC memory questions (scope note N-SCOPE-20260928-01) plus LongMemEval-S for comparability. Metrics: answer accuracy with a grader from a family different from every reader, paired McNemar test per question; extraction cost per 1,000 writes, GPU hours and latency; 3 runs per arm. Predeclared margin: B acceptable if accuracy is within 2 points of A (paired interval lower bound above minus 2). Kill: B more than 2 points below A on PDLC questions. Design warnings from the note: the source's frontier model (gemini-3.7-flash) was also its judge, placement rows were single runs, and the local-versus-frontier split was measured on the 100K setting only.
why it matters for us: The CTO's priority 2 is running decision and memory models ourselves so memory text stays in-house; this is the first seat-by-seat measurement that isolates the extractor, and it says most of the quality lives in the answer seat. If it holds on our data, ingestion can be self-hosted now and the hosted spend confined to answering (R9, with R6 checks on the grading).
