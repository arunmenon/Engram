claim: On our held-out set with injected wrong-answer episodes, REALM-style reconsolidation audited by the same model that answered raises the weight of edges into the node that produced a wrong answer in at least 10% of those episodes, and an auditor from a different model family that strengthens only on externally confirmed success cuts that feedback-loop rate by at least half while staying within 1 point of the same-model arm on knowledge-update accuracy.

proposed experiment: Arm A = reconsolidation off (edge weights static; the REALM ablation baseline). Arm B = REALM-style auditor, same model as the answerer, bounded updates (strengthen w += eta*c*(1-w), weaken w -= eta*c*(w - w_min), eta < 1, non-zero w_min, new edge at c), edges only, node content never rewritten. Arm C = Arm B's update rule with an auditor from a different model family that may strengthen only when a `task.outcome` acceptance label on the warm-up stream confirms the answer, and may only weaken otherwise (the frozen evaluation judge is never the confirmation signal). Dataset: reconsolidation runs on a separate warm-up question stream over the same stores, with 20% of its episodes given a planted wrong answer before auditing and no questions generated from the answer-containing session (REALM generated 1 to 6 extra questions per sample from the oracle session); weights are then frozen and scored on H2's harness over the LongMemEval knowledge-update subset plus our P3 held-out set including abstention questions, which never feed the auditor. Metrics: knowledge-update accuracy, abstention accuracy, feedback-loop rate = fraction of planted wrong-answer warm-up episodes in which any edge into the wrong node was strengthened, and edges saturated at w = 1.0; every weight change logged with its query id. Predeclared margins: B vs A must exceed the noise floor on knowledge-update accuracy for reweighting to stay in H9 at all; C must halve B's loop rate at a knowledge-update cost of at most 1 point. Kill: B's loop rate below 10% (the risk is small and Arm C is unnecessary), or C costs more than 1 point, or neither B nor C beats A beyond the noise floor (REALM's measured gain was +2.01 on LoCoMo and +2.13 on LongMemEval, with +4.17 on knowledge-update, under a protocol that pre-warmed on the oracle session and excluded unanswerable queries).

why it matters for us: R2 asks whether memory should update on read, and the only ablated evidence (REALM) used the same GPT-4o-mini to answer and to audit, never measured reinforcement of wrong memories, and Hippo reports no measurement of unmarked wrong-memory reinforcement either. This card gives B17's unreported feedback-loop rate its first number and decides whether B4's small gain survives an honest protocol before we build read-time reweighting.
