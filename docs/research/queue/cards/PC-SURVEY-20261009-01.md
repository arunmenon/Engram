claim: Deleting a memory record leaves its influence intact when derived summaries and caches survive: memory delete had Any-Leak 0.94, identical to no forget, while replay from the injection point guided by provenance reached 0.00 at about 9x fewer recomputed tokens than a full reset (arXiv 2609.04875, 7 to 8B models).

proposed experiment: On H10's seeded project history, plant 30 target facts (for example a revoked dependency choice) and let Consumer 4 run so they reach Summary and merged nodes. Issue an erase request for each. Arms: (A) delete the target node only, current behaviour; (B) delete target plus every node reachable over derived-from edges, regenerating Summary nodes from surviving sources. Probe with 6 direct and indirect questions per target through /v1/context and a downstream agent task. Metric: fraction of probes whose served context or agent action reflects the erased fact; tokens and wall time of regeneration; utility on 30 non-target questions. Kill: arm A leak rate below 0.10 (the paper's failure does not reproduce on our stack), or arm B costs more than rebuilding the tenant from raw events.

why it matters for us: Erasure is a customer and compliance promise, and the R2 thesis says erasure needs semantics distinct from suppression. We have no measurement of whether our delete removes influence; the paper says a plain delete removes none.
