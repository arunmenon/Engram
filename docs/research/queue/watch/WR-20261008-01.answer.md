what was asked: the re-judge protocol and results behind "3 changed answers in 500" (arXiv 2609.38021 v2), including judge model, prompt and sampling conditions.

answer:

- Judge model: "gpt-4o-2024-08-06", "resolved snapshot exact" (section 8). The harness "records the resolved model snapshot per verdict" (section 2).
- Prompt: the benchmark's LongMemEval rubric templates, SHA-256 pinned ("9c9d67fab129..."), released verbatim. Preference and abstention templates match upstream exactly; base and temporal-reasoning differ by a trailing space; the knowledge-update template differs in substance (two extra sentences). So the judge is an "adapted GPT-4o rubric", not the official template.
- Procedure: "the frozen pass-1 reader checkpoint was re-judged the same day in a clean output directory with the identical harness ... fresh controls 12/12 and 12/12" (positive and negative judge controls, 0.9 pass threshold).
- Result: "478/500: 497 of 500 verdicts agree with the quoted 479; answerable is identical (452/470); the three flips are on byte-identical answer text (7024f17c and 031748ae_abs lost, a2f3aa27 gained)". Two of the three flips are lost credits, one gained; one of the three is an abstention row.
- Sampling conditions for this re-judge: NOT STATED. The paper states "temperature zero, a ten-token output cap, one user message, and no tools" only for the new September 30 knowledge-update repetitions (section 5.6). Nothing in the v2 HTML gives temperature or seed for the historical verdicts or the section 8 re-judge. Treat as unknown.
- Number of re-judges: one. The authors' own scope: "This single re-judge does not establish a universal noise floor or estimate the full judging distribution" (section 5.1) and "neither a reliable rank nor a universal noise band follows" (section 8).
- Related numbers in the same paper: a cross-family second judge (GLM-5.3) agreed on 493/500 in each pass (7 disagreements, five of them abstention rows in pass 1); Claude Sonnet 5.5 as judge disagreed with GPT-4o on 12/500 Opus answers and 7/500 Sol answers; Sonnet re-judging 30 Opus answers changed 2 of 30.

verdict for the renderer spec: it may cite "3 of 500 labels changed on one same-judge re-judge of fixed answers (GPT-4o 2024-08-06, adapted LongMemEval templates, sampling settings not stated)" as ONE observation of within-judge disagreement on chat-memory answers. It may NOT cite it as a noise floor: n = 1 re-judge, sampling unknown, development-set answers, and the authors disclaim that reading. The renderer spec should measure its own judge's repeat-disagreement (at least three repeats at fixed settings on its own answers) rather than borrow this number.
