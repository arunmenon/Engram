claim: On H8's harness with identical, fully specified retrieval mechanics and hard caps, a trivial post-retrieval trigger (run the second retrieval round only when round 1 returns no hits or its top-1 retrieval score is below a floor fitted on the fit split) is non-inferior to the Jev-Mem sufficiency stop with Engram-fit thresholds on answer accuracy (margin 2 points), with mean retrieved tokens and false-sufficiency rate within uncertainty-aware guards, and not worse than fixed k by more than 2 points. If so, B4's pilot candidate needs no decision-model call on the read path.

Sources and what they do and do not show. N-SURVEY-20261003-01 (Router-Mem): a trained yes/no sufficiency router kept 55.17 of 56.49 on AMA-Bench while stopping early on 30.0% of queries, but on BEAM stayed below always-deep at every tau (38.77 or 42.09 vs 43.26); an untrained prompted router was either too eager (BEAM 86.5% early stop, 29.62) or too timid; n not stated, no false-sufficiency rate, no fixed top-k arm, no matched caps, router and judge model unnamed. N-SURVEY-20261003-06 (coverage illusion): an untrained "retrieval returned nothing usable" trigger escalated 27.8% of 1,000 real queries and beat always-escalate on composite 4.084 vs 3.944 at 65.6 s vs 96.2 s; encyclopedia QA, judge-scored, no code. Correction from r1: the "33.6%" figure is not a real-query escalation oracle. In the primary's Table 8 it is the Tier 1 share over 2,206 high-contrast queries pooled across all conditions; the Real User row is 609 queries with Tier 1 47.6% and Tier 2 52.4%. It shares no denominator with 27.8% and is not used here. The note N-SURVEY-20261003-06 repeats the error; a correction request is raised in this run's summary (this agent does not edit notes). Neither source compares the two triggers, which is the gap this spec fills. H8 compares the Jev-Mem stop against fixed k; this spec adds the trivial-trigger arm and asks whether the cheap trigger matches the learned one, so it is not merged; it shares H8's harness and runs in the same window.

shared retrieval mechanics (identical in every arm; frozen in `retrieval.json`):
- round 1: the pinned hybrid retriever returns its top 12 nodes for the question text (the first 12 of K's ranking).
- round 2 (when run): query = question text plus the titles of the top 3 round-1 nodes; the retriever returns its top 24; nodes already served are removed by node id; the first 12 survivors are appended.
- caps: at most 2 rounds, at most 24 nodes, at most 6,000 retrieved tokens; nodes beyond the token cap are dropped in rank order.
- consequence: every adaptive arm serves either the round-1 context S (12 nodes) or the two-round context D (up to 24 nodes). T, J and D are therefore selections between two cached answers per question, which is what makes the fit objective affordable.

arms (same questions, answer model, prompt and caps):
- K (baseline, H8's): one round, the retriever's top 24 nodes (same caps).
- T (decision arm): trivial trigger; serve D when round 1 has no hits or top-1 score is below floor f, else S. f is fitted on the fit split.
- J (decision arm): Jev-Mem sufficiency stop using H8's four conditions, copied verbatim from H8's frozen spec into `j_conditions.json` at freeze (the freeze refuses if H8's text is absent); serve S when all four hold, else D. Its thresholds are fitted on the fit split.
- D (reference): always two rounds.
- fit grid: f over retrieval-score quantiles 0.00 to 0.95 in 0.05 steps (20 values); J's thresholds over a 4 x 4 grid on its two continuous conditions (H8's two boolean conditions fixed as specified). Objective on the fit split: accuracy minus 0.5 points per 1,000 mean retrieved tokens; ties go to the cheaper setting. Fit accuracy for any setting is computed from the cached S and D fit answers, which are graded once.

metric: primary, answer accuracy on the test split (benchmark gold; where the benchmark specifies a model judge, that judge is pinned and must come from a different model family than both the answer model and Jev). Also: retrieved tokens per question, rounds, false-sufficiency rate (arm served S, its answer is wrong, and D's answer is right), escalation rate, p95 added read-path latency, decision-model calls per question.

predeclared margin (outcomes are mutually exclusive by construction):
- let d = accuracy(J) minus accuracy(T), with one-sided 95% cluster-bootstrap bounds lo and hi.
- quality floor (required for positive): one-sided 95% lower bound of accuracy(T) minus accuracy(K) above -0.02.
- guards (required for positive): one-sided 95% upper bound of tokens(T) / tokens(J) at most 1.15; one-sided 95% upper bound of false-sufficiency(T) minus false-sufficiency(J) at most 0.05.
- positive: hi < 0.02 and floor and guards pass.
- negative: not positive, and lo > 0.
- floor_fail: neither positive nor negative, and the floor fails for T or for J (J's floor computed the same way).
- inconclusive: otherwise.
- A small J advantage that is significant (lo > 0) but practically small (hi < 0.02) is therefore classified positive, matching the stated non-inferiority claim.

sample size and power note: 200 fit and 300 test questions, clustered by source conversation or session (about 60 test clusters). At discordance 0.10 and design effect 1.3 the SE of the paired difference is about 2.1 points, so if T and J are truly equal the 2.0-point upper bound clears with probability about 0.25 at n=300. This is a pilot that can return a clear negative or show T is close; `tl power` (cluster resampling, 1,000 draws, seed 20261109) runs before any model call and is recorded; the rule does not change with it. Fit was reduced from 300 to 200 questions to pay for fit-path answers.

kill criterion: negative as above. Invalid: thresholds or floor fitted on any test question; retrieval mechanics or caps differing from `retrieval.json`; answer model, prompt, judge or Jev version differing from the pin; judge from the same family as the answer model or Jev; per-arm failure rate above 3%; fewer than 280 test questions scored in all four arms.

what a pass unlocks: a pilot result for the B4 stop rule (no decision-model call on the read path, lower latency, memory text stays in-house); promotion to production shadow is a separate, reviewed spec. B9 (lead) gains a local measurement separating a learned stop from a trivial one; B13 (lead) gains a borderline-population measurement (questions where T and J disagree are reported as their own population).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): harness extension on H2/H8's harness 2.5 engineer-days (shared retrieval mechanics, S/D caching, T and J selection, false-sufficiency scorer). Model tokens, all calls: fit answers S and D, 200 x (2.5k + 4.0k) = 1.3M; test answers S, D and K, 300 x (2.5k + 4.0k + 4.0k) = 3.15M; Jev conditions on fit and test, 500 x 1.5k = 0.75M; judge calls (only if the benchmark uses a model judge), 1,300 answers x 0.3k = 0.39M; total about 5.6M in 5 shards, hard cap 6M. USD about 33 at the frozen price table with the pinned mid-tier answer model, under 9 per shard. Cost S.
