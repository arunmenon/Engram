claim: On cross-tool engineering records (issue tracker tickets, GitHub issues and pull requests, design and requirement wiki pages, CI test records), deciding whether two records are the same work item, as distinct from two records that are related (one implements, verifies, references or is the parent of the other): (1) a small open-weight cross-encoder or a Jev Noul beats a shared-key and exact-title rule by at least 5 F1 on the same-item class without merging more than 5% of related-but-distinct pairs; (2) Jev does not beat the small cross-encoder by 3 F1 on both the in-distribution and the shift set; (3) a frontier LLM earns an escalation path only if it beats the shipped arm by at least 5 F1 on a held-out source pairing. Source: N-SURVEY-20260928-22 (AnyMatch, arXiv 2409.04073): a 124M matcher averaged 81.96 F1 against 86.36 for GPT-4 (MatchGPT) over 9 generic entity-matching datasets, zero-shot leave-one-dataset-out, at a quoted 3,899 times lower cost per 1K tokens (2024 prices); losses concentrated on heterogeneous product records (for example WDC 63.31 vs 85.83), and the companion study (arXiv 2607.24688) reports cross-encoders ahead of bi-encoders and generative matchers ahead mainly under distribution shift. No source contains engineering records and no typed decision model is in any comparison; the register holds no belief on cross-hub identity, so this decides G13's implementation rather than a grade.

arms (same candidate pairs, same pair rendering: title, type, key fields, first 512 tokens of body, source system):
- A: rules: same item if a shared key links them (a ticket id or issue number in a title, branch or body; a URL to the other record) or titles match exactly after the frozen normalisation (lowercase, strip punctuation and ticket prefixes, collapse whitespace). This rule knowingly merges related records; measuring that is part of the test.
- B: a small open-weight cross-encoder (ModernBERT-base, or the AnyMatch GPT-2 124M checkpoint if its licence permits; chosen and pinned in `freeze.json` before labelling ends), fine-tuned on XHUB-TRAIN with the recipe in `freeze.json` (learning rate, epochs, early stop on XHUB-DEV F1), one seed chosen before test scoring.
- C: Jev Noul `same_item` whose true criterion is the rubric's same-item definition and whose false criterion names the related-but-distinct cases, neutral option names per E-20260928-06-r2's frozen template, pinned version, threshold fitted on XHUB-DEV to maximise F1.
- D: a frontier LLM, zero-shot, one call per pair, pinned model and the rubric text as prompt (sha256 in `freeze.json`).
- Reported, not decided on: every arm on the subset of pairs with no shared key (where the rule cannot help).

metric: F1 on the same-item class on XHUB-TEST-IN and XHUB-TEST-SHIFT (primary); false-merge rate on related-but-distinct pairs (a guard, with a one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson bound); precision at the chosen threshold; cost per 1,000 pairs (C and D at list price on the run date; B at amortised GPU seconds); p95 latency per pair (500 timed pairs, warm, batch 1); whether pair text left our infrastructure (C, D yes; A, B no).

predeclared margin: 5 F1 for an arm over A on both test sets; 3 F1 for C over B on both sets for C to ship instead of B; 5 F1 for D over the shipped arm on the shift set (the card's margins). A shipping arm must also keep its false-merge rate on related-but-distinct pairs at or below 5% (one-sided 95% upper bound). Each arm is judged by its own rule; an arm that fails its own rule never ships, whatever another arm does.

sample size and power note: 1,000 labelled pairs in three classes (about 35% same item, 30% related-but-distinct, 35% unrelated but similar, drawn by blocking so negatives are hard): XHUB-TRAIN 350, XHUB-DEV 150, XHUB-TEST-IN 300 and XHUB-TEST-SHIFT 200. Splits are made by project before any pair is generated, and the shift set is one whole source pairing (for example design wiki page to GitHub pull request) from projects held out of train and dev. At about 105 same-item pairs in test-in and 70 in shift, F1 is estimated to within about +/- 5 to 8 points (95%, bootstrap, estimate); paired differences are tighter because arms share pairs, but a 3-point difference is not resolvable at this size. Near-margin rule: when the 95% interval of a deciding difference contains its margin, that decision is inconclusive; the cheaper, in-house option is kept meanwhile. The related-but-distinct guard at about 90 related pairs per test set bounds a 0-merge result at about 3.3%. Resampling: pairs sharing a record are dependent, so the bootstrap resamples connected components of the record graph (records as nodes, labelled pairs as edges) within each test set; components never span splits because splits are by project.

kill criterion: no model arm (B or C) beats A by 5 F1 on both test sets with its false-merge guard met: ship rules only. C fails to beat B by 3 F1 on at least one set while B passes its own rule: ship B (in-house). D fails to beat the shipped arm by 5 F1 on the shift set: no escalation path. Invalid (rerun under a new spec revision, not a verdict): labeller kappa below 0.7 on the three-class label, any shift-set project or source pairing found in train or dev, or any record id appearing in two splits.

what a pass unlocks: G13's implementation for the federation gateway's identity problem at the linking stage (priority 3), with the self-hosted option first (priority 2), and a guard that keeps identity from collapsing trace links; 1,000 labelled cross-tool pairs that seed a linking family in the PDLC memory benchmark, including the related-but-distinct class that E-20260928-08-r2's trace-link labels need.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): labelling 1,000 pairs by two labellers at about 2 minutes each, about 67 labeller-hours plus adjudication; B fine-tune on one GPU, under 1 GPU-hour (1 run); C and D on 650 pairs (dev plus test) each at about 1.5k tokens per call, about 2.0M tokens (2 runs); under 40 USD in total. Export and blocking from the public sources, 3 engineer-days; harness 3 engineer-days. Cost M.
