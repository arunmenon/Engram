claim: On H2's harness at equal retrieved-token budget, a normalised convex combination of channel scores (BM25, vector, graph) with weights fit on a small dev split beats reciprocal-rank fusion with a shared k = 60 on answer accuracy by more than H2's noise floor; and whether the graph channel shows lift (H2's question) does not change sign between the two fusion functions. Source: arXiv 2210.11934 (N-SURVEY-20260928-08), convex combination beat RRF(60,60) on every dataset reported by about 1.5 to 3 NDCG points (for example MS MARCO 0.425 vs 0.454), weights converging on under 5% of training queries. That study fused two rankers over documents; Engram fuses heterogeneous units and the graph channel may have no usable score, which is part of what is tested. Pillar note: the card tagged R7; the claim tested is about request-time hybrid retrieval, so the spec tags R3 primary with R7 secondary (B23 is an R7 belief).

arms (graph traversal on in A to C, as in H2's graph-on arm; embedder, candidate depth, k served and token budget identical to H2):
- A: RRF with a shared k = 60 (H2's current fusion; reuse H2's graph-on results if run on the identical frozen configuration, otherwise rerun).
- B: RRF with k tuned per channel on the dev split over the grid {5, 10, 20, 40, 60, 100}.
- C: convex combination of per-channel scores after min-max normalisation per query (theoretical min-max where a channel's score range is known), weights fit on the dev split by grid search over the simplex in steps of 0.1, then frozen.
- D: as C with graph traversal off (the H2 graph-off counterpart under the tuned fusion), so the graph lift can be read under both fusions.

metric: answer accuracy and token-F1 at equal retrieved-token budget (H2's metric), served tokens, and p95 retrieval latency.

predeclared margin: C (or B) beats A only if the paired gain exceeds H2's noise floor (spread of at least 5 unchanged runs) and the 95% clustered interval excludes zero. The graph-lift sign test: H2's graph-on minus graph-off difference under RRF k=60 and under the winning fusion (C minus D) are both reported; a change of sign or a change in whether the interval excludes zero is the finding that matters to H2.

sample size and power note: LongMemEval-S has 500 questions plus MemFail long-hop items as frozen by H2; the dev split for tuning is drawn from outside H2's locked held-out set (at most 10% of available non-held-out questions, per the source's under-5% convergence result). The source gains of 1.5 to 3 NDCG points may be smaller in end accuracy; with about 500 paired questions a 3-point accuracy difference is detectable only if discordance is modest (inference); if H2's noise floor exceeds 3 points, this study can only rule out large effects and the verdict says so. Clustering unit: the conversation or haystack instance a question belongs to.

kill criterion: For C: no gain over A beyond the noise floor, or unstable weights (the dev-split optimum's weight vector moves by more than 0.2 in L1 distance across 20 bootstrap resamples of the dev split), or the graph channel lacks a usable score (more than 20% of graph-channel candidates tied at one score, or no per-item score emitted); in that last case the convex arm is run on BM25 and vector only with the graph channel appended by RRF, and the verdict states it. For B: same noise-floor rule.

what a pass unlocks: A fusion baseline chosen on our data rather than by default; a B23 revision; and confidence that H2's decision to keep or demote the graph as a read surface (the plan's stop-and-rewrite point) does not rest on an untested fusion choice.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): three extra sweeps over H2's set (B, C, D) plus dev-split tuning; retrieval is local, reader tokens as in one H2 arm per sweep; about 2 people-days once H2's harness exists. Cost S.
