claim: On our frozen engineering-agent traces, rendered through a provenance allowlist that removes every evaluator-produced outcome, a decision-model pass/fail judge (one binary rubric proposition per trace, threshold fixed on a dev split by a deterministic rule) is non-inferior to our LLM judge on agreement with blinded human labels (95% lower bound of the difference >= -0.05), has failure recall no lower than the LLM judge's, completes at least 98% of calls validly, and flips verdicts on no more traces across repeated calls, at lower all-attempt cost per decision.

arms:
- Arm A (incumbent): the P3 LLM judge, structured output {verdict, confidence, rationale}, pinned model and prompt.
- Arm B: decision-model judge, one yes/no proposition per trace; pass if P(yes) >= tau, where tau is chosen on the dev split by the rule in "Threshold rule" below and frozen in `threshold.json` before any test trace is judged.
- Shared rubric: the same proposition for both arms ("Did the agent complete the task stated in `task` so that its stated acceptance checks would pass?").
- Shared rendering, provenance allowlist: each trace segment carries a `source` tag written by the P3 harness at capture time. Only these sources are rendered: `task` (task statement as given to the agent), `agent_turn` (agent messages and tool calls), `agent_tool_result` (raw output of tools the agent itself invoked during the episode, including tests and CI runs the agent triggered), `agent_diff` (final diff). Always excluded: `evaluator` (post-episode test or CI runs by the harness, hidden acceptance tests and their results), `oracle`, `reward`, `label`, `harness_summary` (any pass/fail or score line appended by the harness), and any segment with no `source` tag. Order and cap: task statement, final diff, agent-triggered test and CI output, then the most recent agent turns until 16,000 tokens. Both arms see a byte-identical payload; capped traces are flagged and analysed as a subgroup.
- Payload checks: after rendering, every payload is scanned for (1) any excluded `source` tag, (2) the trace's oracle id, reward field names and label strings from `labels.jsonl` and `index.jsonl`, (3) the harness's own summary line patterns listed in `leak_patterns.json`. Each check is logged per trace to `payload_checks.jsonl`. A trace that fails any check is excluded from both arms and counted; if more than 2% of traces fail, the run is not runnable (renderer defect, not a result).
- Separation: neither judge model is the agent model that produced the traces, and neither is used as answerer in any run it judges.
- Human labels: every trace labelled pass/fail by one labeller blind to both judges and to the executable oracle result; a second blind labeller on 100 test traces for inter-rater agreement; disagreements adjudicated by a third person. Labellers are not the rubric or threshold author. Labellers see the full trace including evaluator segments only if the labelling protocol says so, fixed in `labelling_protocol.md` before labelling starts; judges never do.

Invalid-call policy (predeclared, both arms):
- A call is invalid if it times out (60 s), returns a transport error, returns output that fails the schema (A: missing or non-enum verdict; B: missing probability), or returns a probability or confidence outside [0, 1] or NaN.
- Each invalid call is retried at most 2 times (3 attempts in total), with the same payload and pins. Every attempt is logged and every attempt's tokens and cost count toward that arm's cost.
- If all 3 attempts are invalid, the judgment is recorded as `invalid`. No trace is dropped. For agreement, `invalid` counts as disagreement with the human label. For failure recall, `invalid` on a human-labelled failure counts as not caught. For flips, `invalid` is a distinct verdict value.
- Valid-completion rate = share of judgments (not attempts) with a valid final result. An arm with valid-completion rate below 0.98 on the test split cannot pass.

Threshold rule (deterministic): candidate thresholds are the midpoints between consecutive distinct P(yes) values on the dev split, plus 0 and 1. Choose the candidate that maximises balanced accuracy against the adjudicated dev labels; among ties, the one with the highest dev failure recall; among remaining ties, the highest tau. `threshold.json` records the candidate list, the chosen tau, the tie-break path taken, a timestamp and its sha256.

Repeat dispatch: the repeat subset is 60 test traces fixed in `index.jsonl` before the run. The test-stage call is call 1 for those traces; the repeat stage makes 9 further calls per trace per arm (calls 2 to 10). Flips are computed over the 10 verdicts per trace. Cost for repeats counts the 9 further calls (plus retries).

metric: primary: agreement with adjudicated human labels on the test split; failure recall = share of human-labelled test failures judged fail. Guards: valid-completion rate per arm; repeatability = count of repeat traces whose verdict takes more than one value over the 10 calls. Secondary: expected calibration error of B's P(yes) and A's confidence on the test split (10 equal-width bins; this is what can move B14); agreement with the executable oracle where one exists (supporting evidence, not ground truth, computed outside the judge processes); all-attempt cost per decision and p50/p95 latency; capped-trace subgroup; retry counts per arm.

predeclared margin: judge_pass requires ci95_low(agree_B - agree_A) >= -0.05 AND (recall_B - recall_A) >= 0 AND ci95_low(recall_B - recall_A) >= -0.10 AND valid_rate_B >= 0.98 AND flips_B <= flips_A AND cost_per_decision_B < cost_per_decision_A (all-attempt cost).

sample size and power note: B21 governs the sizes. Support per split: at least 20 task families overall, no family split across dev and test; test split at least 15 families, of which at least 10 contain at least one human-labelled failure; dev split at least 5 families, of which at least 4 contain a failure; at least 75 human-labelled failures in test and 25 in dev; no single family contributes more than 15% of test traces or 20% of test failures. Agreement: 300 test traces with about 10% paired discordance give an unclustered SE of about 0.018; with a true difference of 0 the lower bound clears -0.05 with about 78% power. Traces within a family are correlated, so the SE is inflated by the square root of the design effect: at a design effect of 1.5 power is about 73%, at 2.0 about 62%. The design effect is estimated on the dev split before the test stage and written to `power_check.json`; if it exceeds 2.0 the spec reports the test as underpowered rather than widening the margin. Failure recall: the SE of the paired recall difference is about 0.05 before clustering, so the recall rule is a guard, not a superiority claim. Repeatability: the 60 repeat traces are drawn with at most 3 per family from at least 20 families. 0 flipped traces of 60 bounds the per-trace flip rate at about 4.9% (one-sided 95%) only under independence between traces; the family-level bound is also reported (0 families with any flip out of F families bounds the family flip rate at about 3/F, e.g. 15% at F = 20), and the verdict cites both.

kill criterion: keep_llm_judge if (recall_B - recall_A) < -0.05 OR ci95_high(agree_B - agree_A) < -0.05 OR valid_rate_B < 0.95 (the decision model is clearly worse on the endpoint that matters for gating, or unusable). Stop before running (not a result) if any not_runnable condition in the execution packet holds.

what a pass unlocks: a judge cheap and repeatable enough to score every change in the P3 harness, which raises how often Track B can measure; a local calibration measurement for B14; a candidate register belief ("a decision-model pass/fail judge matches an LLM judge on our traces at lower cost"). Because the frozen-judge rule forbids changing the judge mid-round, a pass applies from the next locked round. It does not replace the executable oracle where one exists.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): M. Payload capped at 16,000 tokens. Full pass: 400 traces x 2 arms x 16,000 = 12.8M. Repeats: 60 traces x 9 further calls x 2 arms x 16,000 = 17.3M. Retries: allowance of 10% of calls, about 3.0M. Total about 33M tokens. Proposed cap for the desk 40M tokens, 12 wall-clock hours. Human labelling: 400 traces at about 10 minutes plus 100 second labels and adjudication, about 10 people-days; renderer source tagging and leak patterns about 2 people-days. Budget: policy unset.

evidence check: the card's strength (vendor-benchmark) matches N-20260927-05 as verified by the challenger: one team on 834 tau2 customer-service traces; human agreement 88 vs 85 of 100 (inconclusive, 95% CI [-10.9, +5.1] for GPT-5 Mini minus Jev); failure recall 25 vs 17 of 29; flips 3 vs 19 of 100 over 20 repeats; $3.23 vs $19.98; one comparator; threshold and version pins not published. B21 (firm) sizes the test and is not moved; B14 (working) can move through the calibration measurement. ## Revision notes

Review REV-20260927-07 (outcome revise). Each finding and what changed:

1. [leakage] Renderer included test and CI output while the harness forbade oracle exposure. Changed: rendering is now a provenance allowlist on per-segment `source` tags; agent-triggered test and CI output is kept as ordinary evidence, while evaluator runs, hidden acceptance results, oracle, reward, label and harness summary segments (and untagged segments) are excluded. Payload checks for excluded tags, oracle ids, reward fields, label strings and harness summary patterns are logged per trace in `payload_checks.jsonl`; more than 2% failing traces makes the run not runnable.
2. [alternative] No policy for malformed outputs, timeouts, invalid probabilities, retries or missing judgments. Changed: predeclared invalid-call policy (definition, at most 2 retries, all attempts costed, `invalid` scored as wrong for agreement and as missed for recall, no trace dropped); valid-completion rate >= 0.98 is required to pass and < 0.95 kills.
3. [packet] No tie rule for the threshold; repeat calls and cost disagreed. Changed: deterministic threshold rule with a two-step tie-break recorded in `threshold.json`. The test-stage call is call 1 of 10 for repeat traces; the repeat stage makes calls 2 to 10, so dispatch, flip denominator (10 verdicts) and the 9-further-calls cost line agree.
4. [alternative] Family counts did not ensure test and failure-bearing clusters; power and flip bounds ignored within-family dependence. Changed: per-split family and failure-bearing family minimums and family share caps, all in `not_runnable`; power is restated with a design effect (73% at 1.5, 62% at 2.0), the design effect is measured on dev before test, and the result is marked underpowered above 2.0; the repeat subset is spread over at least 20 families with at most 3 per family, and both per-trace and per-family flip bounds are reported.
5. [packet] Missing harness, commit, pins, frozen data, fitter, metric script, budget config, egress clearance, manifest; policy unset. Partly changed: the packet now names every file the run must produce and makes each missing item (including a null dollar cap) a machine-checked not_runnable condition. override: the harness, commit, populated pins and frozen data cannot exist before the spec is accepted and the work is scheduled, and caps cannot be authorised while policy.md is unset; the spec says explicitly that it authorises nothing and replaces no judge by itself.
6. [injection] Embedded instructions in the source's quoted conversation. No change needed: noted in the evidence check; nothing from that text is used.
