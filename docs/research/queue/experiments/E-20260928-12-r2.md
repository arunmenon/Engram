claim: This is a pilot. An open-weight decision model fine-tuned with soft labels on about 3,000 memory decisions is non-inferior to zero-shot Jev on held-out projects, judged separately for store/skip and for supersedes decisions, at the card's 2.0-point accuracy margin (one-sided 95% upper bound of Jev minus open model below 2.0 points per decision type; Brier no worse by more than 0.01), with p50 latency at or under 150 ms for 20 questions per state on one GPU. The primary open model starts from the od1-base checkpoint, as the source did, so what is measured is incremental adaptation on our decisions, not reproduction of the Open Decider pre-training. Source: N-20260928-14 (od1-typed-decisions, Hugging Face revision 3e203a7, Apache-2.0): od1-base (Qwen3.5-4B already fully fine-tuned with the Open Decider recipe) adapted on 5,400 in-domain typed decisions in 0.21 H100-hours scored 0.796 against Jev 1.13 at 0.741 (0.727 on the benchmark card) on a 2,000-decision test split, with p50 10.7 ms for one question and 126.1 ms for 20 questions on one H100; the two-model cascade escalated 96.5% of requests and saved little. Caveats that make our test necessary: specialist against zero-shot generalist on the specialist's own workflows, teacher-derived labels, 94 of 400 test states sharing at least half their 13-grams with training, a test split reused for model selection, and no memory-stage decision tested.

arms (same test items, same state rendering, same question wording; option names follow E-20260928-06-r2's frozen template so this result does not depend on its outcome):
- A: hosted Jev, pinned version (jev-1.13.0 or the version pinned at freeze), zero-shot, our production question wording for store/skip (Noul) and supersedes (Noul over a pair of memories).
- B4-od1 (primary): od1-base checkpoint, pinned revision, adapted with the od1 recipe (cross-entropy against soft targets, validation early stop, bf16) on MEMDEC-ASF-TRAIN only. Prior Open Decider training is not ours and not costed here; only adaptation cost is reported.
- B4-raw (secondary, reported, not deployable from this run): the same adaptation recipe starting from the raw Qwen3.5-4B base, to show how much of B4-od1's result depends on the Open Decider pre-training.
- B08 (secondary, reported, not deployable from this run): the same recipe on raw Qwen3.5-0.8B.
- C (reported for B13, not decided on): cascade, B08 first, escalate to A when the lowest per-question confidence is below a threshold fitted on validation; escalation rate and accuracy on the escalated (borderline) population reported separately.
- Controls: majority-class rate per decision type, and Jev after one temperature refit on validation (so a Brier gap is not only a calibration offset).

metric: accuracy per decision type (primary, decided per type, never pooled); Brier; KL to the soft test labels; ECE (15 bins) before and after one temperature refit on validation; p50 and p95 latency for 1, 5 and 20 questions per state; cost per 1,000 decisions (A at list price on the run date; B at amortised GPU seconds); adaptation GPU-hours per model and seed; whether decision text left our infrastructure (A yes, B no).

predeclared margin: the card's 2.0 accuracy points, per decision type: one-sided 95% upper bound of (A minus B4-od1) below 2.0, and Brier(B4-od1) minus Brier(A) at most 0.01, for that type. Absolute floor per type: B4-od1 accuracy at least 10 points above the majority-class rate. Latency ceiling 150 ms p50 at 20 questions. The margin is the tolerated loss the card set; it is not widened for sample size, and the test is labelled a pilot instead.

sample size and power note: test = 1,200 decisions per decision type (2,400 in total) from at least 10 projects never used in training or validation, each double-labelled and adjudicated. If the paired discordance rate is about 0.12 with a design effect of 1.5 for project clustering, the SE of the per-type accuracy difference is sqrt(0.12 x 1.5 / 1200) = 1.22 points, so the one-sided upper bound is the observed difference plus 2.0 points. If the models are truly equal, the bound falls below 2.0 about half the time (the observed difference must be below zero); if B4-od1 is truly 1 point better, about 80%. That is why the verdict has an inconclusive branch and why this is a pilot: a positive is trusted only when the bound clears the margin; a negative needs the point estimate more than 4 points below; everything between is inconclusive and sizes the confirmatory set from the measured discordance. With about 10 test clusters a wild cluster bootstrap is reported alongside the pairs bootstrap.

kill criterion: per decision type, point estimate of (A minus B4-od1) above 4.0 points on unseen projects: negative for that type. B4-od1's 20-question p50 latency above 150 ms: negative for both types. Invalid (rerun under a new spec revision, not a verdict): labeller agreement (Cohen's kappa) on the test set below 0.5 for either decision type, any test project found in training or validation after the fork and overlap checks, or any seed choice made after test scoring.

what a pass unlocks: the priority 2 goal in R9 terms for the decision types that pass: a decision model we run ourselves so memory text stays in-house; a fair basis to compare Jev with other open-weight models on memory decisions; a local calibration measurement for both models under B14 (working); a borderline-population cascade measurement under B13 (lead).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): labelling: 3,000 training items single-labelled plus 600 (20%) double-labelled for agreement, 400 validation single-labelled, 2,400 test items double-labelled (4,800 assignments), about 360 adjudications: 8,800 assignments plus 360 adjudications, about 155 labeller-hours at 1 minute each (corrected from the first spec, which omitted the 600 training double labels and sized the test at 1,200 in total). Executor runs: 3 seeds x 3 models of adaptation on one GPU (B4-od1 and B4-raw about 0.3 to 1.5 GPU-hours each by estimate; the source's 0.21 H100-hours applies only to B4-od1, because it started from od1-base), grouped into 5 runs of at most 2 hours; A on 2,800 validation and test states plus 3 repeats of 200 states for a re-run floor, about 2.5M tokens, 2 runs; scoring 1 run. Total about 8 runs, under 60 USD (GPU rent at list price plus Jev). Fixture construction by T1 (Engram ingestion of about 24 public projects) is a separate T1 job and is not counted here. People: 6 engineer-days for extraction, training and harness. Cost M.
