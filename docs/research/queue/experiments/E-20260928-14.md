claim: On cross-tool engineering records (Jira issues, GitHub issues and PRs, Confluence requirement pages, CI test records), deciding whether two records are the same work item: (1) a small open-weight cross-encoder or a Jev Noul beats a shared-key and exact-title rule by at least 5 F1; (2) Jev does not beat the small cross-encoder by 3 F1 on both the in-distribution and the shift set; (3) a frontier LLM earns an escalation path only if it beats the best of the two by at least 5 F1 on a held-out source pairing. Source: N-SURVEY-20260928-22 (AnyMatch, arXiv 2409.04073): a 124M matcher averaged 81.96 F1 against 86.36 for GPT-4 (MatchGPT) over 9 generic entity-matching datasets, zero-shot leave-one-dataset-out, at a quoted 3,899 times lower cost per 1K tokens (2024 prices); losses concentrated on heterogeneous product records (for example WDC 63.31 vs 85.83), and the companion study (arXiv 2607.24688, abstract only) reports cross-encoders ahead of bi-encoders and generative matchers ahead mainly under distribution shift. No source contains engineering records and no typed decision model is in any comparison; the register holds no belief on cross-hub identity, so this decides G13's implementation rather than a grade.

arms (same candidate pairs, same pair rendering: title, type, key fields, first 512 tokens of body, source system):
- A: rules: same item if a shared key links them (a ticket id or issue number in a PR title, branch or body; a URL to the other record) or titles match exactly after normalisation.
- B: a small open-weight cross-encoder (ModernBERT-base, or the AnyMatch GPT-2 124M checkpoint if its licence permits; chosen and pinned before freeze), fine-tuned on XHUB-TRAIN, early-stopped on XHUB-DEV.
- C: Jev Noul `same_item` (neutral option names per E-20260928-06's frozen template), pinned version, threshold fitted on XHUB-DEV to maximise F1.
- D: a frontier LLM, zero-shot, one call per pair, pinned model and prompt.
- Reported, not decided on: every arm on the subset of pairs with no shared key (where the rule cannot help).

metric: F1 on the "same item" class on XHUB-TEST-IN and XHUB-TEST-SHIFT (primary); precision at the chosen threshold; cost per 1,000 pairs (C and D at list price on the run date; B at amortised GPU seconds); p95 latency per pair; whether pair text left our infrastructure (C, D yes; A, B no).

predeclared margin: 5 F1 for B or C over A (on both test sets); 3 F1 for C over B (required on both sets for C to ship); 5 F1 for D over max(B, C) on the shift set (the card's margins). These are decision rules on point estimates, set so that ties go to the cheaper, in-house option; intervals are reported.

sample size and power note: 800 labelled pairs, 40% same item: XHUB-TRAIN 300, XHUB-DEV 100, XHUB-TEST-IN 250 and XHUB-TEST-SHIFT 150, where the shift set is one whole source pairing (for example Confluence page to GitHub PR) excluded from train and dev. The card specified 400 evaluation pairs; override: B needs its own training pairs and C its own threshold split, so 400 more are labelled rather than reusing test pairs. Precision of F1 at 100 positives (test-in) is roughly +/- 5 to 7 points (95%, bootstrap, estimate), and at 60 positives (shift) roughly +/- 7 to 9; paired differences between arms are tighter because arms share pairs, but a 3-point difference is not resolvable at this size. The rules therefore burden the more expensive or out-of-house arm: a point estimate short of the margin keeps the cheaper choice. Candidate pairs come from blocking (shared tokens or embedding neighbours), so negatives are hard; pairs sharing a record are cluster-bootstrapped by record.

kill criterion: B and C both fail to beat A by 5 F1 on either test set: ship rules only. C fails to beat B by 3 F1 on either set (that is, on at least one of the two): ship B (in-house). D fails to beat max(B, C) by 5 F1 on the shift set: no escalation path. Invalid (rerun, not a verdict): labeller kappa below 0.7, or any shift-set source pairing found in train or dev.

what a pass unlocks: G13's implementation for the federation gateway's identity problem, at the linking stage (priority 3), with the self-hosted option first (priority 2); an escalation rule keyed on unseen source pairings if D earns it; 800 labelled cross-tool pairs that seed a linking family in the PDLC memory benchmark.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): labelling 800 pairs by two labellers at about 2 minutes each, about 55 labeller-hours plus adjudication; B fine-tune on one GPU, under a few GPU-hours; C and D on 500 pairs (dev plus test) each; export and blocking from the four tools, 3 engineer-days; harness 3 engineer-days. Cost M.
