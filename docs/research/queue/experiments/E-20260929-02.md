claim: On labelled engineering-agent turns, write-gate thresholds fitted on our own labels beat both published fixed configurations on held-out save/skip F1 by at least 3 points: jevmem (content at least 0.5, importance at least useful, chit-chat below 0.5) and AI Maestro (category not none, durable at least 0.75, importance at least 2.5 of 4, with a correction bypass at correction at least 0.75 and durable at least 0.5). The arms differ only in the decision rule applied to one cached set of Jev answers. Source: N-20260929-11 (AI Maestro at b2e1928, repo-pinned): the gate exists in code with those defaults, has no stated rationale for its values, and no evaluation of its decisions exists (backlog F007 is Todo); the jevmem configuration is the comparison in that note. Evidence strength: the note shows two builders shipping different untested thresholds for the same gate shape; it measures nothing, so the spec tests whether local fitting buys anything, which B14 (working) assumes. Hosted Jev is not bit-reproducible on identical requests (N-20260929-06: probability SD about 0.01, answer flips near a threshold), so a sub-measurement reports how often each rule's decision flips on repeat calls.

arms (same pinned Jev version; one batched request per turn with the union of both tools' questions, answered once and cached: durable (noul, AI Maestro text), content (noul, jevmem text), category with none (choice, AI Maestro's 7 options), importance (score, 5 levels), correction (noul, AI Maestro text), chit-chat (noul, jevmem text), injection (noul, jevmem text); question texts copied from each repository at its pinned commit and hashed):
- A (jevmem rule): save if P(content) >= 0.5 and round(E[importance]) >= Useful and P(chit-chat) < 0.5.
- B (AI Maestro rule): save if (P(correction) >= 0.75 and P(durable) >= 0.5) or (top category != none and P(durable) >= 0.75 and E[importance] >= 2.5).
- B-nobypass (reported): B without the correction clause, to measure what the bypass adds on corrections.
- C (fitted): B's rule form, with thresholds chosen on the fit fold from the grid: durable 0.30 to 0.90 by 0.05; importance 1.0 to 3.5 by 0.25; correction bypass off, or on with correction threshold 0.50 to 0.90 by 0.10 and durable floor 0.30 to 0.60 by 0.10; category read as the top-class option probability, required to be at least one of none, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.70 or 0.80 when not none (option probabilities, not the `confidence` field, per PC-20260929-01's merge into E-20260926-03-r2). Selection by F1 in 5-fold session-grouped cross-validation inside the fit fold; ties go to the rule with fewer active terms.

metric: save/skip F1 with save as the positive class (primary), from 2-fold cross-fitting grouped by session: fit on fold 1 and test on fold 2, then swap, pooling the held-out predictions; recall on corrections of an earlier decision; false-save rate on chit-chat turns; false-save rate on the planted-injection slice (reported); kind accuracy on saved turns (reported); repeat-call flip rate per rule (reported).

predeclared margin: F1(C) minus F1(better fixed rule) at least 3.0 points, where the better fixed rule is chosen on each fit fold (never on test), and the two-sided 95% interval of (C minus A) and of (C minus B) each excludes zero. Bounded negative: the upper end of the 95% interval of (C minus better fixed) below 3.0 points.

sample size and power note: 300 labelled turns from at least 40 sessions, with at least 40 corrections of an earlier decision and at least 40 chit-chat turns; cross-fitting uses all 300 as test exactly once. At a C-versus-fixed discordance of about 0.12 and a session design effect of about 1.3, the SE of the F1 difference is about 2.5 points. At a true 6-point gain, P(positive) is about 0.67; at a true 3-point gain about 0.23; at a true difference of 0, the bounded negative is reached with probability about 0.22. A small true effect will mostly read as inconclusive, which is declared, and the inconclusive default is operational. Injection slice: 60 planted lines; zero false saves in 60 bounds the rate at about 5% (B21); showing below 2% needs about 149 independent cases, which H10b owns. override: the card's "150 injected lines, rate near 2%" is not run here, because the injection gate is H10b's question and a 150-line planted set would double the labelling for a reported endpoint.

kill criterion: fitting is dropped (fixed_rule_suffices) on the bounded negative. A failed positive without the bounded negative is inconclusive. Invalid (rerun under a new spec revision, not a verdict): labeller kappa on save/skip below 0.6; any threshold chosen using a test-fold turn; a session split across folds; the Jev version or any question sha256 differing from the pin; fewer than 30 corrections or 30 chit-chat turns in the frozen set.

what a pass unlocks: the write-gate default for the PDLC memory (priority 1, the stage the CTO ranked first), with a measured answer to whether local fitting (B14, working) is worth its labels, a measured value for the correction bypass, and a repeat-call flip rate at each rule's thresholds that tells the receipt design how often a re-queried gate decision differs; 300 labelled turns reused by E-20260929-03 as its write-gate family and seeding the write-gate family of the PDLC memory benchmark (priority 4).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): session writing by T1, 3 engineer-days; labelling 360 turns by two labellers, about 15 labeller-hours; harness 2 engineer-days. Model tokens: 360 turns x about 2.5k = 0.9M; repeat calls for turns within 0.05 of any rule threshold, about 70 turns x 4 = 0.7M; total about 1.6M Jev tokens, 2 runs, under 1 USD at 0.042 USD per million; cap 5 USD.
