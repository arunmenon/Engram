claim: On H8's harness with identical hard caps, a trivial post-retrieval trigger (continue to a second retrieval round only when the first round returns no hits or its top hit score is below a floor fitted on the fit split) is non-inferior to the Jev-Mem sufficiency stop with Engram-fit thresholds on answer accuracy (margin 2 points) at no more than 10% more retrieved tokens, and its false-sufficiency rate is no worse by more than 3 points. If so, B4 needs no decision-model call on the read path.

Sources and what they do and do not show. N-SURVEY-20261003-01 (Router-Mem): a trained yes/no sufficiency router kept 55.17 of 56.49 on AMA-Bench while stopping early on 30.0% of queries, but on BEAM stayed below always-deep at every tau (38.77 or 42.09 vs 43.26); an untrained prompted router was either too eager (BEAM 86.5% early stop, 29.62) or too timid; n not stated, no false-sufficiency rate, no fixed top-k arm, no matched caps, router model unnamed. N-SURVEY-20261003-06 (coverage illusion): an untrained "retrieval returned nothing usable" trigger escalated 27.8% of 1,000 real queries (oracle 33.6%) and beat always-escalate on composite 4.084 vs 3.944 at 65.6 s vs 96.2 s; encyclopedia QA, judge-scored, no code. The card's numbers match both notes. Neither note compares the two triggers, which is the gap this spec fills. H8 already compares Jev-Mem stop against fixed k; this spec adds the trivial-trigger arm and an always-deep reference and asks a different question (does the cheap trigger match the learned one), so it is not merged; it shares H8's harness and fit/test split and runs in the same window.

arms (same questions, same retriever, same answer model and prompt, same hard caps: at most 2 rounds, at most 24 nodes, at most 6,000 retrieved tokens):
- K (baseline, H8's): fixed k = max_nodes, one round.
- T (decision arm): trivial trigger; round 2 only when round 1 has no hits or top-1 retrieval score is below floor f, f fitted on the fit split as the lowest grid value (0.01 steps) that maximises fit-split accuracy minus 0.5 points per 1,000 mean retrieved tokens.
- J (decision arm): Jev-Mem sufficiency stop, H8's four conditions with thresholds fitted on the fit split by the same objective; stop after round 1 when the conditions hold.
- D (reference): always-deep, both rounds always.

metric: primary, answer accuracy on the test split (benchmark gold, frozen judge where the benchmark specifies one); retrieved tokens per question; rounds per question; false-sufficiency rate = share of questions where the arm stopped after round 1, its answer is wrong, and D's answer is right; escalation rate; p95 added read-path latency; decision-model calls per question.

predeclared margin: positive (T suffices) needs accuracy(J) minus accuracy(T) with one-sided 95% upper bound below 2.0 points, mean tokens(T) at most 1.10 x tokens(J), and false-sufficiency(T) minus false-sufficiency(J) at most 0.03 (point estimate). Negative (J needed) needs accuracy(J) minus accuracy(T) with one-sided 95% lower bound above 0. K and D are reported; H8's own verdict (J vs K) is computed by H8, not here.

sample size and power note: 300 test questions and 300 fit questions, clustered by source conversation or session (about 60 test clusters). Paired accuracy discordance between T and J is expected to be low (both stop on the same easy questions); at discordance 0.10 and design effect 1.3 the SE of the paired difference is about 2.1 points, so if T and J are truly equal the 2.0-point upper bound clears with probability about 0.25 at n=300. This is therefore a pilot that can return a clear negative (J clearly better) or show T is close; `tl power` (cluster resampling, 1,000 draws, seed 20261109) runs before any model call and is recorded; the rule does not change with it.

kill criterion: negative (T insufficient, B4 keeps J) if the one-sided 95% lower bound of accuracy(J) minus accuracy(T) is above 0. Invalid: thresholds or floor fitted on any test question; caps differing between arms; answer model, prompt or Jev version differing from the pin; per-arm failure rate above 3%; fewer than 280 test questions scored in all four arms.

what a pass unlocks: the B4 stop-rule implementation choice (no decision-model call on the read path, lower latency, memory text stays in-house); B9 (lead) gains a local measurement that separates the value of a learned stop from a trivial one; B13 (lead) gains a borderline-population measurement (questions where T and J disagree are reported as their own population).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): harness extension on H2/H8's harness 2 engineer-days (T arm, D arm, false-sufficiency scorer). Model tokens: answers on test only, 4 arms x 300 x about 3.5k = 4.2M; Jev conditions on fit and test, 600 questions x about 1.5k = 0.9M; round-2 retrieval has no model cost; total about 5.1M in 5 shards, hard cap 6M. USD about 30 at the frozen price table with the pinned mid-tier answer model, under 9 per shard. Cost S.
