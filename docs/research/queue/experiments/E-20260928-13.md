claim: On a seeded PDLC history, deterministic preconditions written as graph predicates on five Action types (merge PR, close ticket, mark requirement verified, supersede decision, relax constraint) catch at least 70% of seeded precondition violations with at most a 5% one-sided 95% upper bound on false blocks, and at least 20 recall points more than an agent that sees retrieved memory only; a typed decision model checking one prose precondition per action adds at least 10 recall points at no more than 800 ms p95. Sources: N-SURVEY-20260928-23 (StepGuard, arXiv 2608.24777: a 4B guard at 83.0 / 83.3 trajectory Acc / F1 against GPT-5.4 at 83.0 / 84.4, 599.9 ms per call, with GPT-5.4 also the label teacher; AgentSpec, arXiv 2503.18666: deterministic predicates at 1.11 to 2.83 ms, over 90% of unsafe executions prevented on 750 RedCode-Exec cases, LLM-written rules at 95.56% precision and 70.96% recall), N-SURVEY-20260928-17 and N-SURVEY-20260928-21 (Palantir action types with submission criteria and a failure message per condition; an executable-ontology implementation), N-SURVEY-20260928-26 (the synthesis proposing G14). Every source tests safety (harm, injection, unsafe code), not lifecycle preconditions; no source measures a PDLC precondition check, which is the question and a novelty claim, hence review.

arms (same 200 proposed actions, same graph snapshot, same retrieved memory for the agent):
- A: no check. A frozen agent (pinned model, temperature 0) receives the proposed action and the memory retrieved for it by the Track B retriever and answers proceed or stop with a reason.
- B: deterministic graph predicates attached to each Action type, evaluated on the graph snapshot; block if any predicate fails, returning the failing condition as the message.
- C-jev: B plus a Jev Noul on the one prose precondition per action ("respects the linked decision's rationale"), with the linked decision text and the proposed change as state; block if B blocks or the Noul answers "violates" above a threshold fitted on a 40-action dev split.
- C-open: as C-jev, with an open-weight 4B model (Intern-Decision 4B, or a 4B step guard if its licence permits, chosen and pinned before freeze) in place of Jev.
- Reported, not decided on: B evaluated on a degraded graph with 20% of IMPLEMENTS and VERIFIES edges removed at random (3 seeds), because the predicates are only as good as the links (the linking stage is tested in E-20260928-08).

metric: violation recall (primary), overall and split into predicate-expressible violations and prose-only violations; false blocks on benign actions with a one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper bound; added p95 latency per check; for A, the agent's stop rate on violating and on benign actions.

predeclared margin: B recall at least 0.70 and B false-block upper bound at most 5% on at least 120 benign actions; B recall minus A stop-rate-on-violations at least 0.20; C recall minus B recall at least 0.10 with C p95 added latency at most 800 ms (the card's margins).

sample size and power note: 200 actions, 80 violating at least one precondition and 120 benign, labelled blind by two engineers. False blocks: with 120 benign actions the one-sided 95% upper bound is 2.5% at 0 false blocks, about 3.9% at 1 and about 5.2% at 2, so B passes the 5% bound only with at most 1 false block. Recall at n = 80: SE at 0.80 is sqrt(0.8 x 0.2 / 80) = 0.045, so the 0.70 threshold is resolvable from a true recall of about 0.80 or above with a margin of about 2 SE. B versus A (paired on the same 80 violations): at a discordance rate of 0.35 the SE of the difference is sqrt((0.35 - 0.2^2) / 80) = 0.062, so a true 20-point gap is observed above 0.20 about half the time; the rule uses the point estimate and the interval is reported, so near-margin results are labelled inconclusive. C versus B is decided on prose-only violations, which are at most a subset of 80; the 10-point rule is directional and reported with its interval. Clustering: violations built from the same seed record are correlated; intervals are cluster-bootstrapped by seed record.

kill criterion: B recall below 0.70 or B false-block upper bound above 5%: drop predicates as the primary mechanism. C-jev and C-open both add fewer than 10 recall points over B, or both exceed 800 ms p95: G14 is rules only. B recall minus A below 0.20: the check adds nothing over good retrieval, drop the Action type from the ADR-0011 amendment. Invalid (rerun, not a verdict): labeller kappa on violating or benign below 0.6, or any predicate edited after cases were built.

what a pass unlocks: the kinetic half of the ontology (priority 3): Action types with preconditions and failure messages in the ADR-0011 amendment, a new gateway verb that can say "stop, and here is why", and, if C passes, a System One job at the federation layer that is not in G1 to G13; the 200 labelled actions become the "warn before acting against a known requirement" family of the PDLC memory benchmark (priority 4). B25 (firm) is not re-graded by this test; the test shows whether the product can enforce it at action time.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): predicate writing for five actions, 2 engineer-days; case construction and blind labelling of 200 actions by two engineers, about 5 engineer-days; agent runs for A (200 calls), Jev and open-weight calls for C (400 calls plus the 40-action dev split), the open model on one GPU; harness 4 engineer-days on top of H7's seed. Cost M.
