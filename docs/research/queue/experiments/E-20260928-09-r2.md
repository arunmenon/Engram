claim: On H2's harness at equal retrieved-token budget, one tuned fusion function, selected on a history-disjoint dev split before any held-out evaluation from {B: RRF with per-channel k, C: normalised convex combination of channel scores}, beats reciprocal-rank fusion with a shared k = 60 on answer accuracy by more than H2's frozen noise floor; and, as a separate confirmatory test, the graph lift (graph on minus graph off) differs between the selected fusion and RRF k = 60 (a paired fusion-by-graph interaction). Source: arXiv 2210.11934 (N-SURVEY-20260928-08), convex combination beat RRF(60,60) on every dataset reported by about 1.5 to 3 NDCG points (for example MS MARCO 0.425 vs 0.454), weights converging on under 5% of training queries. That study fused two rankers (lexical and semantic) over documents; Engram fuses heterogeneous units and the graph channel may have no usable score, which is part of what is tested. The source gives no estimate of end-accuracy gains or of any fusion-by-graph interaction; power below is computed from our sample, not from the source. Pillar note: the card tagged R7; the claim tested is about request-time hybrid retrieval, so the spec tags R3 primary with R7 secondary (B23 is an R7 belief; B8 is the R3 graph-lift belief the interaction test can move).

arms (a 3 x 2 design: three fusion functions, each with graph traversal on and off; embedder, candidate depth per channel, k served, served-token budget, reader and judge identical to H2):
- A_on: RRF with a shared k = 60 over BM25, vector and graph (H2's graph-on arm). A_off: RRF k = 60 over BM25 and vector (H2's graph-off arm). Reused from H2 only if H2's run_manifest matches this spec's code pin, dataset manifest sha256 and configuration hash exactly; otherwise rerun here.
- B_on: RRF with k tuned per channel on the dev split over the grid {5, 10, 20, 40, 60, 100} for each of k_bm25, k_vec, k_graph (216 points). B_off: k_bm25, k_vec retuned on the dev split with graph off over the same grid (36 points).
- C_on: convex combination of per-channel normalised scores, weights (w_bm25, w_vec, w_graph) fit on the dev split by grid search over the simplex in steps of 0.1 (66 points), then frozen. C_off: weights for BM25 and vector retuned on the dev split with graph off (11 points: w_bm25 in {0, 0.1, ..., 1}, w_vec = 1 - w_bm25). Predeclared weight handling: the retuned C_off is the primary graph-off counterpart (it answers "does the graph add lift over the best graph-off hybrid of this fusion family", which is H2's question); C_off_renorm (C_on's weights with w_graph dropped and the remaining two renormalised to sum 1) is run and reported as a secondary, descriptive arm only and cannot trigger any decision.
- Fusion rules (frozen): RRF score = sum over channels c of 1/(k_c + rank_c), a candidate absent from channel c contributes 0. Convex: vector cosine normalised with theoretical bounds [-1, 1]; BM25 and graph normalised by per-query min-max over that channel's candidate list at H2's candidate depth; if a channel's max equals its min for a query, every candidate in that list gets 1.0; a candidate absent from a channel gets 0 for that channel. Ties in fused score are broken by A's rank, then by candidate id (lexicographic). Served-token truncation is applied after fusion identically in every arm.
- Unusable graph score: the graph channel is unusable for convex fusion if any graph candidate lacks a numeric score, or more than 20% of pooled graph-channel candidates on the dev split share the modal score. This is checked on the dev split before tuning. If unusable, C is withdrawn from the candidate set (recorded as "convex not applicable to the graph channel as scored"), B is the selected fusion by default, and no hybrid fallback is run. The former fallback (convex BM25 and vector with graph appended by RRF) is removed.
- Selection: before any held-out evaluation, the selected fusion F* is whichever of B and C has the higher dev objective (ties go to B, the smaller change from A). Only F* is tested confirmatorily; the non-selected arm is run and reported descriptively only.

metric: answer accuracy (primary) and token-F1 at equal retrieved-token budget (H2's metric), served tokens, and p95 retrieval latency. Dev tuning objective (frozen, retrieval-only, uses no reader or judge): mean evidence recall at the served-token budget, meaning the fraction of a dev question's gold answer-bearing session or turn ids (as annotated in the H2 manifest) present in the served context; ties broken by token count served (fewer wins), then by the grid point closest to A (k = 60 for all channels; equal weights for convex). Dev items without evidence annotations in the H2 manifest are excluded from the dev split.

predeclared margin: Noise floor estimator (frozen): run A_on unchanged 5 times on the held-out set, varying only the seeds H2 leaves free (reader sampling and any retrieval nondeterminism); noise_floor = max minus min of the 5 run-level accuracies, in percentage points. It is written to noise_floor.json and its sha256 recorded in the run manifest before any B or C held-out result is computed. H2's 5 unchanged runs are reused only on an exact manifest and code-pin match. Two confirmatory tests on the held-out set, Bonferroni-adjusted (alpha = 0.05 / 2 = 0.025 each, so 97.5% two-sided cluster-bootstrap intervals): (1) main effect: F*_on beats A_on only if mean paired accuracy difference > noise_floor and the lower bound of its 97.5% interval > 0; (2) interaction: per question, D_i = (F*_on_i - F*_off_i) - (A_on_i - A_off_i); the graph lift depends on fusion only if the 97.5% interval of mean D excludes 0. A change in the sign of the graph lift, or in whether separate intervals exclude zero, is reported but is not by itself a finding.

sample size and power note: LongMemEval-S has 500 questions plus MemFail long-hop items as frozen by H2; held-out n is H2's locked held-out count (at most the full pool; 500 is used below as the LongMemEval-S reference size, and the harness recomputes with the actual n). Dev split: drawn only from non-held-out items, at most 10% of available non-held-out items (per the source's under-5% convergence result), after history disjointness filtering. Paired binary accuracy: per-question difference in {-1, 0, 1}, variance = p_d - d^2, where p_d is the discordance rate (share of questions on which the two arms disagree) and d the true difference. Required n = (z_{1-alpha/2} + z_{power})^2 x (p_d - d^2) / d^2 with alpha = 0.025 two-sided (z = 2.2414) and power 0.80 (z = 0.8416): (2.2414 + 0.8416)^2 = 3.0830^2 = 9.505. For d = 3 points: p_d = 0.05 gives 9.505 x 0.0491 / 0.0009 = 519 questions; p_d = 0.10 gives 9.505 x 0.0991 / 0.0009 = 1047; p_d = 0.20 gives 9.505 x 0.1991 / 0.0009 = 2103. So at n = 500 the main effect minimum detectable difference (solving d = 3.083 x sqrt((p_d - d^2)/500)) is about 3.1 points at p_d = 0.05, 4.3 at p_d = 0.10 and 6.1 at p_d = 0.20. The interaction contrast D_i is a difference of two paired differences; taking its variance as about 2 p_d (no positive correlation between the two graph lifts assumed, conservative), MDE at n = 500 is about 4.3 points at p_d = 0.05, 6.1 at p_d = 0.10 and 8.6 at p_d = 0.20. Clustering: if questions share a conversation or haystack instance, variance is inflated by the design effect 1 + (m - 1) x ICC (m = questions per cluster); if each question has its own haystack, m = 1 and the design effect is 1. The harness reports actual n, cluster count, observed p_d, design effect and achieved MDE for both tests in verdict.json. Consequence: this study can rule out only fusion or interaction effects larger than the achieved MDE (or the noise floor, whichever is larger); a null at n = 500 does not show fusion is irrelevant to H2, and the verdict says so. Clustering unit: the conversation or haystack instance a question belongs to.

kill criterion: Preconditions (not run, status stays queued): policy.md status is unset or no approved token, cost and wall-clock caps exist; H2 harness or manifest is missing. For C: graph score unusable (C withdrawn as above), or unstable weights (the dev optimum's weight vector moves by more than 0.2 in L1 distance across 20 bootstrap resamples of the dev split, resampled by cluster), in which case C cannot be selected. For B: unstable k (the dev-optimal k vector changes in any channel across more than 10 of the 20 bootstrap resamples) means B cannot be selected. If neither B nor C is selectable, the study stops before held-out evaluation with verdict "no stable tuned fusion; keep RRF k=60". For F*: main effect not above the noise floor with interval lower bound above 0 is the negative outcome. Budget: halt immediately if any approved cap is reached; the partial results are reported as inconclusive.

what a pass unlocks: A fusion baseline chosen on our data rather than by default; a B23 revision; and, if the interaction test is significant, evidence that H2's decision to keep or demote the graph as a read surface (the plan's stop-and-rewrite point) depends on the fusion choice, which moves B8's reading; if the interaction is not significant with an achieved MDE small enough to matter, confidence that H2's graph verdict does not rest on an untested fusion choice.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): held-out answering sweeps, counted in full: 5 unchanged A_on (noise floor; one doubles as the A_on arm), A_off, B_on, B_off, C_on, C_off, C_off_renorm = 11 sweeps with no reuse, or 6 if H2's 5 unchanged runs and A_off match exactly. Reader plus judge tokens = sweeps x T_H2, where T_H2 is the token cost of one H2 arm over the held-out set (taken from H2's manifest; not known yet). Dev tuning is retrieval-only: per-channel candidate lists for graph on and off are cached once on the dev split, then all 329 grid points (216 + 36 + 66 + 11) and the 20 bootstrap resamples per arm are CPU-only recomputations with no reader tokens. About 4 people-days once H2's harness exists (estimate: 2 in the original plus the added graph-off arms, split construction, isolation checks and interaction statistic). Cost S to M.
