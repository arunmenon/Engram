claim: An open-weight decision model (Qwen3.5 base at 0.8B and 4B) fine-tuned with soft labels on about 3,000 of our own memory decisions is non-inferior to zero-shot Jev on held-out repositories for store/skip and supersedes decisions (accuracy difference, Jev minus open model, with a one-sided 95% upper bound under 3.0 points; Brier no worse by more than 0.01), at a p50 latency at or under 150 ms for 20 questions per state on one GPU. Source: N-20260928-14 (od1-typed-decisions, Hugging Face revision 3e203a7, Apache-2.0): a 4B model fine-tuned on 5,400 in-domain typed decisions in 0.21 H100-hours scored 0.796 against Jev 1.13 at 0.741 (0.727 on the benchmark card) on a 2,000-decision test split, with p50 10.7 ms for one question and 126.1 ms for 20 questions on one H100; the two-model cascade escalated 96.5% of requests and saved little. Caveats that make our test necessary: the comparison is specialist against zero-shot generalist on the specialist's own workflows, labels are teacher-derived, 94 of 400 test states share at least half their 13-grams with training, the test split was reused for model selection, and no memory-stage decision was tested.

arms (same test items, same state rendering, same question wording; option names follow E-20260928-06's frozen template so this result does not depend on its outcome):
- A: hosted Jev, pinned version (jev-1.13.0 or the version pinned at freeze), zero-shot, our production question wording for store/skip (Noul) and supersedes (Noul over a pair of memories).
- B4: Qwen3.5-4B fine-tuned with the od1 recipe as published on the card (cross-entropy against soft targets, validation early stop, bf16), on MEMDEC-OD-TRAIN only.
- B08: the same recipe on Qwen3.5-0.8B.
- C (reported for B13, not decided on): cascade, B08 first, escalate to A when the lowest per-question confidence is below a threshold fitted on validation; report escalation rate and accuracy on the escalated (borderline) population separately, as B13 requires.
- Controls: majority-class rate per question type, and Jev after one temperature refit on validation (so a Brier gap is not only a calibration offset).

metric: accuracy per decision type and pooled (primary); Brier; KL to the soft test labels; ECE (15 bins) before and after one temperature refit on validation; p50 and p95 latency for 1, 5 and 20 questions per state; cost per 1,000 decisions (A at list price on the run date; B at amortised GPU seconds); whether decision text left our infrastructure (A yes, B no).

predeclared margin: non-inferiority margin 3.0 accuracy points for B4 (and separately B08) against A, one-sided 95% upper bound of (A minus B) below 3.0, with Brier(B) minus Brier(A) at most 0.01; latency ceiling 150 ms p50 at 20 questions. override: the card proposed 2.0 points; at the test size we can label this quarter the upper bound sits about 2.0 points above the observed difference (see power note), so a 2.0 margin would pass only if the open model were observed ahead, which would make the test about superiority. Whether the 2.0 bound is also met is reported.

sample size and power note: test = 1,200 decisions from at least 8 repositories never used in training or validation, about 600 per decision type; each test item labelled by two people and adjudicated. If the paired discordance rate is about 0.12, the unclustered SE of the accuracy difference is sqrt(0.12 / 1200) = 0.0100; with a design effect of 1.5 for repository clustering it is about 0.0122, so the one-sided 95% upper bound is the observed difference plus 1.645 x 1.22 = 2.0 points. If the true difference is 0, the probability that the bound falls below 3.0 is Phi(1.0 / 1.22) = Phi(0.82), about 79%; if the true difference is 1.0 point, about Phi(0) = 50%. The latency ceiling is checked on 500 timed states per question count. With 8 test clusters the repository bootstrap is coarse; a wild cluster bootstrap interval is reported alongside.

kill criterion: point estimate of (A minus B4) above 4.0 points on unseen repositories, or B4's 20-question p50 latency above 150 ms (the card's kill rules); B08 is judged by the same rules but a B08 failure alone does not kill the claim. Invalid (rerun, not a verdict): labeller agreement (Cohen's kappa) on the test set below 0.5 for either decision type, or any test repository found in training data.

what a pass unlocks: the priority 2 goal in R9 terms, a decision model we run ourselves for two memory stages so memory text stays in-house; a fair basis to compare Jev with other open-weight models on our own decisions; a local calibration measurement for both models under B14 (working); a borderline-population cascade measurement under B13 (lead).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): labelling about 5,800 labels (3,000 training items single-labelled with 20% double-labelled for agreement, 400 validation single-labelled, 1,200 test double-labelled plus adjudication), at about 1 minute per decision, about 100 labeller-hours; fine-tuning 2 models x 3 seeds on one GPU, under 5 GPU-hours total if the card's 0.21 GPU-hours for 5,400 decisions carries over (estimate); Jev calls on 1,600 items (validation plus test) plus 3 repeats on 200 items for a re-run floor; 6 engineer-days for extraction, training and harness.
