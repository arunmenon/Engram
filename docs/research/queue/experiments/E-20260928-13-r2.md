claim: On a seeded PDLC history, deterministic preconditions written as graph predicates on five Action types (merge PR, close ticket, mark requirement verified, supersede decision, relax constraint) catch at least 70% of predicate-expressible precondition violations with at most a 5% one-sided 95% upper bound on false blocks, and at least 20 recall points more than a frozen agent given either retrieved memory (the card's comparison) or exactly the graph facts the predicates read (the matched-information control); a typed decision model checking one prose precondition per action adds at least 10 recall points on prose-only violations at no more than 800 ms p95 and without breaking the false-block bound. Sources: N-SURVEY-20260928-23 (StepGuard, arXiv 2608.24777: a 4B guard at 83.0 / 83.3 trajectory Acc / F1 against GPT-5.4 at 83.0 / 84.4, 599.9 ms per call, with GPT-5.4 also the label teacher; AgentSpec, arXiv 2503.18666: deterministic predicates at 1.11 to 2.83 ms, over 90% of unsafe executions prevented on 750 RedCode-Exec cases, LLM-written rules at 95.56% precision and 70.96% recall), N-SURVEY-20260928-17 and N-SURVEY-20260928-21 (action types with submission criteria as a product shape; an executable-ontology implementation; the per-condition failure-message detail is unverified, see revision notes), N-SURVEY-20260928-26 (the synthesis proposing G14). Every source tests safety (harm, injection, unsafe code), not lifecycle preconditions; no source measures a PDLC precondition check, which is the question and a novelty claim, hence review.

arms (same 240 proposed actions, same frozen graph snapshot):
- A: no check, retrieved memory. A frozen agent (pinned model, temperature 0) receives the proposed action and the memory retrieved for it by the Track B retriever and answers proceed or stop with a reason. This is the card's baseline.
- A-graph: no check, matched information. The same frozen agent receives the proposed action plus a rendering of exactly the nodes and edges that B's predicates for that Action type read (the predicate read-set, logged by B), and answers proceed or stop. This separates the value of the check from the value of seeing the graph facts.
- Q: generic graph lint, no Action types. One action-agnostic rule set over the touched artifacts (block if any linked Requirement is unverified, any verifying Test fails, or any active Constraint touches the artifact), run on every action regardless of type. This separates typed per-action preconditions from a plain structured query.
- B: deterministic graph predicates attached to each Action type, evaluated on the graph snapshot; block if any predicate fails, returning the failing condition as the message.
- C-jev: B plus a Jev Noul on the one prose precondition per action ("respects the linked decision's rationale"), with the linked decision text and the proposed change as state; block if B blocks or the Noul answers "violates" above a threshold fitted on the 40-action dev split.
- C-open: as C-jev, with an open-weight 4B model (Intern-Decision 4B, or a 4B step guard if its licence permits, chosen and pinned in `freeze.json` before any case is built) in place of Jev.
- Reported, not decided on: B on a degraded graph with 20% of IMPLEMENTS and VERIFIES edges removed at random (3 seeds), because predicates are only as good as the links (linking is tested in E-20260928-08-r2 and E-20260928-18).

metric: B and Q recall on predicate-expressible violations (primary for B); C recall on prose-only violations (primary for C, the only denominator for the C rule); false blocks on benign actions for every blocking arm, with a one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper bound; added p95 latency per check; for A and A-graph, the agent's stop rate on each violation class and on benign actions.

predeclared margin: B recall on predicate-expressible violations at least 0.70 and B false-block upper bound at most 5% on 140 benign actions; B recall minus A stop rate and B recall minus A-graph stop rate, both on predicate-expressible violations, at least 0.20 each; B recall minus Q recall reported (Action types earn their keep over a generic lint if at least 0.10, reported, not a kill); a C arm passes if its recall on prose-only violations exceeds B's by at least 0.10, its combined false-block upper bound stays at most 5%, and its p95 added latency is at most 800 ms. override: the card measured B's 70% on all violations; prose-only violations are by construction outside what predicates can express, so B's rule is judged on predicate-expressible violations and C's on prose-only violations, each with its own fixed denominator.

sample size and power note: 240 test actions: 60 predicate-expressible violations, 40 prose-only violations, 140 benign, plus a separate 40-action dev split (used only for C thresholds). Labelled blind by two engineers; each violation tagged expressible or prose-only before any arm runs, and the build continues until both minimums (60 and 40) are met. False blocks: with 140 benign actions the one-sided 95% upper bound is 2.1% at 0 false blocks, 3.3% at 1, 4.4% at 2 and 5.4% at 3, so a blocking arm passes with at most 2 false blocks. B recall at n = 60: SE at 0.80 is 0.052. B minus A or A-graph (paired on 60 violations) at a discordance rate of 0.35: SE about 0.071. C minus B on 40 prose-only violations: B catches none of them by construction, so C's recall itself is the difference; SE at 0.30 is 0.072. Near-margin rule: when the 95% cluster-bootstrap interval of a deciding quantity contains its margin, that decision is inconclusive rather than pass or fail. Clustering: violations built from the same seed record are correlated; intervals are cluster-bootstrapped by seed record.

kill criterion: B recall on predicate-expressible violations below 0.70 with the interval's upper end below 0.70, or B false-block upper bound above 5%: drop predicates as the primary mechanism. B minus A-graph below 0.20 with the interval's upper end below 0.20: the check adds nothing over serving the same facts, drop the Action type from the ADR-0011 amendment. Both C arms fail their rule: G14 is rules only. Invalid (rerun under a new spec revision, not a verdict): labeller kappa on violating or benign below 0.6, fewer than 60 expressible or 40 prose-only violations, or any predicate edited after cases were built.

what a pass unlocks: the kinetic half of the ontology (priority 3): Action types with preconditions and failure messages in the ADR-0011 amendment, a gateway verb that can say "stop, and here is why", and, if a C arm passes, a System One job at the federation layer that is not in G1 to G13; the 240 labelled actions become the "warn before acting against a known requirement" family of the PDLC memory benchmark (priority 4). B25 (firm) is not re-graded; the test shows whether the product can enforce it at action time.

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): predicate and lint writing, 2 engineer-days; case construction and blind labelling of 280 actions by two engineers, about 6 engineer-days; executor: A and A-graph 480 agent calls at about 4k tokens each (about 1.9M tokens), C-jev and C-open on 280 actions (about 0.4M tokens, the open model on one GPU), predicates and lint free; 3 runs, under 30 USD; harness 4 engineer-days on top of the PDLC-SEED-ASF fixture.
