claim: For typing items from connected engineering tools onto the ontology's shared types and properties, a bind-time mapping per source field (a small encoder retrieves the top 5 shared properties, a typed Choice picks one; items are then typed by lookup) is no more than 3 points of type accuracy below a serve-time typed Choice per item once unbound fields fall back to serve-time typing, and is either at least 1 point more accurate or at least 50 ms p95 cheaper at serve time; and after a schema change (two renamed custom fields in the issue tracker) the deployed binding with a breaking-change check silently mistypes no more than 5% of items. The bind-time arms are tested twice: fully automatic, and human-assisted (a person confirms each field mapping once), so the gain of binding is separated from the gain of human review. Sources: N-SURVEY-20260928-24 (Magneto, arXiv 2412.08194, VLDB 2025: the best combined system, a fine-tuned MPNet retriever with a LLaMA3.3-70B reranker, reached 0.860 MRR on GDC, against 0.731 for a zero-shot configuration that also differs in reranker and serialisation, so the gap is a whole-system comparison, not a retriever-training effect; COMA++ about 0.35, read from a figure; LLM-only schema-to-ontology mapping, arXiv 2609.13776, fell from 0.79 at 23 classes to 0.51 at 300; a column type annotator lost about 5 points on an unseen serialisation, arXiv 2512.22742), N-SURVEY-20260928-17 and N-SURVEY-20260928-18 (Palantir and Fabric IQ type data through a binding made once per source), N-SURVEY-20260928-26 (the survey synthesis recommending G7 move to bind time). None of these sources is on engineering tools and no typed decision model is in any comparison; the register holds no belief on source typing, so this decides G7's design rather than a grade.

arms (same 300 served items, same gold labels, same ontology version):
- A: serve-time G7: a Jev Choice per item per key field over the fixed tag sets, pinned version, neutral option names per E-20260928-06-r2's frozen template. No human input.
- B-auto: bind-time mapping, automatic: for each source field, a small encoder (MPNet-class, pinned) embeds the field name, description and 5 sample values and retrieves the top 5 shared properties; a Jev Choice picks one of the 5 (or "none"); items are typed by lookup. Fields mapped to "none" stay unbound and untyped.
- C-auto: B-auto plus arm A's serve-time Choice applied only to unbound fields.
- B-human and C-human: as B-auto and C-auto, but one engineer confirms or corrects each field mapping once, with minutes logged. The confirming engineer sees only the field name, description and the 5 frozen sample values, never the evaluation items or gold labels, and does not label.
- Sample values for every bind-time arm are drawn by a frozen, seeded rule from a pool of records disjoint from the 300 scored items.
- Schema change (applied after the main run): rename two custom fields in the issue-tracker export; re-run every bind-time arm with and without the migration rule (unknown field name or changed field id raises a binding-breaking flag and routes the field to serve-time typing until re-bound).

metric: type accuracy on served items (primary: key fields mapped to the gold type and property); serve-time p95 latency and cost per 1,000 items; one-off binding effort (engineer minutes per source, human arms only); after the schema change, silent mistype rate (items typed wrongly with no flag) with and without the migration rule.

predeclared margin: a bind-time arm is eligible for deployment only if its accuracy is at most 3 points below A and its post-change silent mistype rate with the migration rule is at most 5%; it is worth deploying only if it is at least 1 point above A or at least 50 ms p95 cheaper than A. The selected arm is the eligible, worth-deploying arm with the least human input (C-auto before C-human, B before C only if B is itself eligible). override: the card reads "B or C more than 3 points below A: keep serve-time". B and C are alternative deployments, not a joint system, so each is judged on its own rule and a failure of one does not block the other; the card's intent (no deployed arm may be more than 3 points below A) is kept exactly.

sample size and power note: 300 served items from three backends (100 each from three issue-tracker projects, three GitHub repositories and three design wiki spaces, pooled per backend), with at least 40 distinct key fields per backend (at least 120 fields), gold type and property per key field labelled blind by two people. Under bind-time arms every item from one source field shares the field's mapping, so the effective sample is the number of distinct fields, and intervals are cluster-bootstrapped by source field. At 120 fields and a paired discordance rate of about 0.10, the field-level SE of an accuracy difference is about sqrt(0.10 / 120) = 0.029, a 95% half-width of about 5.7 points. Near-margin rule: when the 95% interval of (arm minus A) contains minus 3 points, that arm's accuracy rule is inconclusive, not pass or fail. The silent mistype rate after the change is a proportion over the items from the two renamed fields and is reported with a Clopper-Pearson interval; the 5% rule is judged on the point estimate and flagged inconclusive when the interval spans 5%. The latency comparison is well powered (bind-time is a lookup; A is a model call per item).

kill criterion: every bind-time arm more than 3 points below A with the interval's upper end below minus 3: keep G7 at serve time. The only accuracy-eligible arms exceed 5% silent mistypes after the change even with the migration rule: keep serve-time until the binding's breaking-change check is redesigned. A within 1 point of the selected arm and the selected arm saves under 50 ms p95: keep serve-time only. Invalid (rerun under a new spec revision, not a verdict): labeller kappa on type below 0.7, fewer than 40 distinct key fields in any backend, any sample value drawn from a scored item, or the confirming engineer found to have seen evaluation items.

what a pass unlocks: the one gateway change the survey recommends: the ontology as a versioned per-backend contract with G7 at bind time (priority 3), with a measured answer to whether a person must confirm bindings, a retriever in front of the typed Choice once the type set grows, and a breaking-change rule for bindings; design input for the binding registry (components 15 and 17).

cost (runs, tokens, people-days): labelling 300 items' key fields by two people, about 3 labeller-days; binding confirmation for the human arms, under 1 engineer-day for 120 fields (logged as upkeep); executor: arm A's Jev calls on 300 items x key fields and the bind-time Choices on 120 fields, about 1.0M tokens, retriever on one CPU or GPU, schema-change replay; 2 runs, under 20 USD. Exports and schema-change preparation 2 engineer-days; harness 3 engineer-days. Cost M.
