## Contradictions (first)

1. **"Palantir backs objects by source datasets without moving them" is not what Palantir documents.** Its Object Data Funnel "orchestrates writes into the Ontology by reading from datasources and user edits, then indexing this data into object databases". That is copy and index by default. Virtual tables (pointers to outside platforms) can back objects, but the pages read do not say whether those objects are also indexed. Fabric IQ likewise materialises its instance graph on a scheduled refresh. The only mature design found that federates by linking rather than copying is OSLC (tools keep resources at their own URIs), plus OpenBKN's claim of data virtualisation. This contradicts a premise in the focus question, not a register belief. It matters because our integrate-not-absorb rule means we cannot adopt Palantir's backing model as is. (N-SURVEY-20260928-17, -18, -19)
2. No register belief (B1 to B25) is contradicted. B25 is supported by the action and precondition evidence.

## R10: what I looked for, what I found

**Looked for:** focus questions 1 to 3, covering Palantir's Ontology from its own docs, other 2025 to 2026 framings, lifecycle standards, and the ontology as the federation contract.

**Found and written:**
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-17, Palantir Foundry Ontology** (vendor docs, 12 pages). The semantic half is object types, links and interfaces. The kinetic half is action types with submission criteria and a failure message per condition, writeback and an action log. Branches and proposals are "analogous to a Pull Request", and a breaking schema change cannot be saved without an edit migration. **We lack the whole kinetic half, and the mapping part of the semantic half.**
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-18, Fabric IQ ontology** (vendor docs, preview). Bindings are a separate artifact from types; relationships carry `confidence` and `effectiveAt`; instances record their source and when they were true.
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-19, OSLC RM, CM and QM plus OpenLineage, SLSA and in-toto** (standards). The standard names cover most of our PDLC links. There is nothing for Decision or PR as types. in-toto's digest-bound subject is a standard form for our content-hash anchoring.
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-20, Graphiti and Cognee** (vendor docs). Both let you choose prescribed or learned types (Cognee's modes are annotate and strict). There is no measurement and no admission gate.
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-21, OpenBKN** (repo, self-reported numbers with no baseline). Object, Relationship, Risk, Action, with risk types gating actions. The gate itself is unmeasured.
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-25, EDC and AutoSchemaKG** (papers). Canonicalising proposed types into existing ones keeps the type count down at higher precision. No paper ablates an admission gate on its own.
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-26, synthesis "A new way to look at our ontology"**. It answers the four questions in reader order.
  - Our call: add Action types, interfaces, OSLC-named links and schema versioning with migrations to ADR-0011.
  - The one gateway change: make the schema registry a versioned per-backend binding contract, and move G7 to bind time.
  - Do not copy hand modelling, copy-and-index, or permissions that stop at export.

**Folded into the synthesis, no separate note (below the bar):**
- Foundation Capital context graphs essay and follow-up: opinion, no evidence.
- Open Semantic Interchange / Apache Ossie: a draft spec, no evidence, weak fit.
- ReqIF: search snippets only.

## R9: what I looked for, what I found

**Looked for:** focus question 4, evidence for a System One or small model at each ontology decision.

**Found and written:**
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-22, AnyMatch and the companion factorial study** (G13). A 124M matcher reached 81.96 F1 against GPT-4's 86.36, at "3,899 times lower" cost. Cross-encoders beat bi-encoders. **Strong evidence for small models, none on engineering records, no Jev row.**
- **N-SURVEY-20260928-23, StepGuard, AgentSpec and ShieldAgent** (proposed G14, the action check). A 4B guard matches GPT-5.4 at 599.9 ms. Rules cost 1 to 3 ms, but rules written by an LLM reached only 70.96% recall. **Strong on safety, nothing on PDLC preconditions.**

- **Builder reports** of Jev used for ontology typing, cross-tool identity or action checks: none found.
